构建 AIE：与 swyx 的开放问答和反馈环节

工作坊的排队长龙、不稳定的 Wi-Fi 和一个让人沮丧的应用，暴露出 AI Engineer 大会背后的实际取舍。这场开放讨论解释了世界博览会在优化什么、哪些地方需要修正，以及讲者、本地组织者和学者可以如何参与。

01

一个 AI 工程大会的议程里应该有什么？

Should AI Engineer have an AI safety or existential-risk track? The first question at this World's Fair feedback session puts the program's selection criteria under pressure. swyx, one of AIE's organizers, starts with a practical dividing line: content should help people do something they would use at work. Broader discussion, research and theory usually fall outside that remit, although attendee-organized meetups can make room for subjects absent from the official tracks. AI for science already supplied one such example.

An attendee challenges that distinction: engineers building systems that run in ongoing loops should also consider where those systems lead. The response makes the engineering connection more concrete. Security, sandboxing, guardrails and evaluations already put control over AI into everyday development. Andon Labs' Vending-Bench, which opened the evaluations track, becomes swyx's example of studying alignment through systems interacting with the real world. His preference is to uncover problems through that work; his claim that it discovers more alignment issues than abstract existential-risk discussion is a judgment, rather than a comparison established here.

02

四天的展会服务于日程各不相同的人

The next request sounds straightforward: shorten an expo that felt nearly empty on its final day. The attendee proposes two expo days, with other time reserved for workshops, conference sessions or networking. Yet other attendees had complained about the opposite problem: the expo opened only at 4 p.m. on Monday, rather than running all day. Those conflicting requests reveal different ways of using the same event.

The World's Fair is meant to support an expo-only visit: walk around, encounter companies and get a sense of the field. Keeping it open across Monday through Thursday lets someone with work commitments attend on whichever day they can manage. Continued operation also depends on people staffing booths, activity taking place and sponsors paying for it. The more curated summits serve a different purpose, with greater emphasis on a focused content program.

An alternative under consideration would expand the expo idea upstairs: a content expo alongside the sponsor expo, allowing conversations without everyone racing between timed talks. That would reduce schedule pressure but introduce another cost—standing around all day is tiring. Even the spaces intended as breaks carry competing preferences. The music corner delights some attendees and interrupts others' quiet time; the leadership lounge and downstairs expo offer different environments. There is no single atmosphere that satisfies everyone.

03

通过本地合作伙伴成长，而不让组织者成为瓶颈

A request to return to Shift in Croatia opens a discussion of how AIE itself developed. Shift helped inspire the conference, and its organizers provided mentorship. AIE now also occupies space in the travel plans and budgets of speakers, sponsors and attendees who attend other conferences. That makes international expansion a question of where AIE fits, as well as how many events it can run.

An attendee from Bangalore asks whether India will get an AIE event. About 40 countries, swyx says, have expressed interest in some form of local AIE. The organizing model borrows the TED/TEDx distinction: a central event alongside locally run events that can serve their own communities. Local partners supply knowledge and organizing capacity that the central team lacks. The constraint is that the AIE name should continue to mean good technical content and meaningful conversations. Bangalore is a likely starting point for India, rather than an announced, settled event.

This does not require a new online community platform. AIE's emphasis is in-person participation, supported by existing social networks, blogs and local meetups. Italy and London already have recurring groups. Another Slack would add somewhere else to check; existing platforms already contain the people and conversations. Posting there also helps others discover the events. The intended community can extend beyond people who identify exclusively with AIE.

04

可靠的线下聚会比生产级的打磨更重要

The Minneapolis meetup organizer asks how to raise production quality. The answer starts with the recurring work that makes a meetup happen at all: finding speakers, securing a venue, ordering food and assembling volunteers. A stable venue and a predictable date—every fourth Thursday, for example—give attendees a habit they can return to. Sharing the work matters more than making one organizer responsible for everything.

Several choices can then improve the experience independently:

- Format: Social time, two talks and more social time is a reasonable default. A beer-garden gathering also counts as a meetup; every gathering need not become a miniature conference with two to five presentations. - Speaker selection and recording: Better speakers and thoughtful recording can add polish, but recording the entire event is optional. - Discovery and publishing: A website, social account and YouTube channel can make the group recognizable enough that visiting speakers know where to go. Sponsorship can help, too, but each addition creates work.

JS.LA is offered as an example of a well-run, well-produced meetup. The practical priority is event organization and publishing—the “80/20” in swyx's formulation—while keeping the entry barrier low. A group can add more ambition later. Running a conference, however, can become a full-time job, which changes the commitment substantially.

05

满员的工作坊需要尽早给门外的人一个答复

One attendee spent half the workshop day waiting for rooms they never entered. The capacity problem is simple to describe and difficult to allocate around: rooms are roughly equal in size, while demand for their sessions is unequal. Recordings preserve access to the material, but they do not recover the time spent in a queue. Moving to a second or third choice only works if an attendee learns soon enough that the first choice is unavailable.

Workshop preregistration had been promised and did not ship. The paid mobile-app vendor's implementation did not fit the team's needs; swyx acknowledges the failure, while noting that he was not personally involved in diagnosing it. Registration also introduces no-shows: a reserved seat does not necessarily become an occupied seat. The next version therefore needs to improve admission and communicate rejection quickly, rather than treating a signup form as the whole solution. No completed reservation or no-show policy is presented here.

06

先稳住场地，再改进它的运作方式

Will the conference keep growing? Size has helped attract top speakers, but the announced intention for 2027 is “same size, same venue.” Moscone's layout is now familiar to sponsors, attendees, speakers and the organizing team. Reusing it makes better allocation of existing space possible, including areas on level three that remained underused. The same week is booked for the following year. Growth in geographic access can continue without moving the San Francisco event into a larger venue.

The goal is an industry institution that lasts decades. That favors improving the experience for both attendees and staff over another immediate increase in scale. Staying in San Francisco is a firm preference; a longer stay at Moscone is an intention, while the suggestion of raising money to build a new venue is delivered as a joke.

Wi-Fi exposes a harder infrastructure limit. Attendees report workshop demonstrations failing, and the team received complaints and refund demands despite buying the venue's maximum offered package. swyx attributes the mismatch to engineering workloads: many laptops downloading gigabytes can overwhelm connectivity designed for other conference uses. That explains the proposed workload mismatch, without identifying a measured network bottleneck.

The existing mitigations serve different users. Speakers have separate Wi-Fi, though not every speaker may have been told about it; sponsors also have a separate network for demonstrations. Moscone permits cellular tethering where the previous Marriott basement location had poor reception. Attendee congestion remains unresolved. Supplying additional hotspots is floated as a possibility, rather than a selected or sized solution.

A follow-up about Shanghai illustrates the dependency in the international partner model. At the time of the session, November was the target, the date had already moved twice, and the event remained tentative—hence the waitlist. External partners determine whether those plans can become an event. The purpose is reciprocal exchange: bring American researchers and engineers to meet Chinese counterparts, complementing visits in the other direction.

07

社交时间与公开的演讲资料库相互竞争

Leadership attendees bring experience in organization design, product and management that could help one another. An attendee asks for more time to exchange it through open-ended discussions. A lounge permits those conversations, but the request is for the program to actively make room for them. swyx's response gives attendees more responsibility: they can skip sessions, use the lounge and arrange meetings afterward.

Behind that disagreement is a second audience. Recorded content can reach people who never enter Moscone, and a talk that draws a small room may perform much better online. Filling a paid-for room with a useful recorded presentation therefore has value even when most attendees choose something else. Leaving the slot empty would create a common networking period, but would give up that recording opportunity.

London's leadership luncheons offer a more structured alternative. Five to ten minutes of guided discussion introduced roughly three topics, followed by conversation across tables for the rest of the hour. The prompts supplied a shared starting point without consuming the whole session. Participants reviewed the format favorably, and swyx sees it as something to bring back at summits.

08

开放的日程数据便于实验，默认应用依然重要

A request for a location filter turns into a larger product decision. Attendees want to separate booth activities from the agenda and stop scrolling through sessions that have already ended. swyx wants to replace the dedicated vendor app with a web app the team controls, so these changes can be implemented directly. This is an intention for the following year, rather than a completed replacement.

AIE had already published schedule data as JSON and provided MCPs and CLIs, allowing attendees to build their own interfaces. That approach accommodates different preferences without requiring the organizer to predict every useful view. It also leaves a clear obligation: most attendees still need a good default. After three years and seven AIE events, swyx feels he understands the requirements well enough to take more control of that experience.

What can an attendee-built interface do with the schedule? A brief exchange describes one experiment with an automatic tour through the talks, removing the need to navigate each item manually. Its useful distinction is the navigation behavior: the same conference information can support an exploratory experience as well as a conventional agenda. The exchange does not explain the implementation or establish how well it works.

09

深度内容是有的，但参会者得找得到

Twenty minutes can show what a product does without giving enough time to understand how it works. An attendee contrasts short presentations with hour-long workshops that reveal the work in practice. The correction is that AIE already mixes lengths, although the attendee had not encountered the longer sessions. Dedicated workshop rooms accept longer material; the first day has ten workshop tracks, with three workshop rooms across the remaining days.

GDC supplies an inspiration for deep sessions: spend an hour on one specific implementation problem, such as a game's web-slinging mechanic. Shorter talks serve quick introductions and force condensation. swyx also favors their fit for online viewing, while reserving longer slots for material he believes earns the time. The disagreement is partly about duration and partly about discoverability: a mixed program cannot help someone who never finds its longer sessions.

Track continuity raises a related tension. A four-hour sequence benefits from consistent depth, fewer repeated introductions and topics that build on one another. A standalone YouTube talk needs enough context to make sense by itself. AIE manually clusters and sequences material—for example, MCP talks within the context engineering track—but speaker availability can defeat the ideal order. Playlists provide another opportunity to arrange the material after recording. The preferred unit remains a self-contained talk.

10

导览和录像解决的是信息过载的不同部分

The first New Engineer Orientation, or NEO, came from feedback at the previous conference. It helped, but left practical questions unanswered: would lunch be served, and how should someone manage the schedule? Organizing it added work while the team was already struggling to prepare the main event. The result leaned toward networking instead of full onboarding. An online attendee guide existed, but the attendee had not seen it, and sending email did not ensure the information reached everyone.

Keeping the next event at the same venue and time gives the team a year to improve those processes around an experience that 7,000 people have now encountered. The first orientation moved the conference from none to some; its next version needs to answer operational questions as well as introduce people.

Another attendee describes a different failure mode: so many appealing simultaneous talks that choosing becomes exhausting. Once two talks are already being missed, staying at the expo and watching everything later at 2× begins to feel reasonable. Recordings preserve the presentations, but the attendee particularly values asking speakers questions afterward. That opportunity disappears when the live session is missed.

swyx recognizes the FOMO but keeps the World's Fair's breadth as a deliberate choice. The recording library makes missed content recoverable, and single-track summits offer an alternative for people who want fewer simultaneous decisions. The World's Fair is intended as the large showcase where people bring their best work. That preserves abundance while accepting some discomfort; it does not fully answer the attendee's concern about losing direct access to speakers.

11

应用的具体问题变成明年的需求

The detailed app feedback starts with reproducible actions rather than a general dislike. One Android attendee reports crashes after leaving and reopening the app. Opening a talk and returning to the schedule can produce several seconds of loading; another report adds a jump back to the top, losing the browsing position. Loading also interrupts scrolling. Fetching more talks is the attendee's hypothesis for that pause, not an established diagnosis. The observable requirement is to preserve navigation state and avoid interrupting routine browsing.

The schedule problems divide into several distinct behaviors:

- Personal views: “My Schedule” should behave like a filtered version of the main schedule, retaining search and filters. Instead, it lacks those controls and starts at the first day, forcing users past completed sessions to reach today. - Saving live sessions: Once a session has started—even by one minute—the attendee cannot save it. That prevents bookmarking one ongoing session while attending another. - Cross-device continuity: The web and mobile experiences do not synchronize saved choices. swyx explains that they use different codebases; the requested outcome is one consistent schedule regardless of interface. - Usable locations: Some booth activities show only “booth” as their location. A description may supply the number and company, but entries without that extra text leave attendees unable to find the activity.

More features are not automatically the priority. The attendee sees little use of embedded chat and Q&A and suggests removing them, while finding the filters cumbersome. A more useful addition might recommend people to meet based on interests or roles, such as founders or people working in big tech. The leadership track already had a prototype: researched attendee profiles distributed to participants. swyx wants to invest further in that direction, based on seeing matching work elsewhere, while remaining skeptical of conference chat.

The attendee prefers the website's navigation, which swyx controls directly. Taking ownership of event technology is nevertheless a separate discipline from organizing the event. The recorded complaints become a requirements document for the proposed replacement. Open data remains available for independent experiments, but a suggested app marketplace has only a handful of submissions so far. Making customization possible has produced some participation, rather than universal adoption.

12

一个团队可以围绕大会打造自己的学习计划

A participant from a sports nonprofit explains a use of the conference that extends beyond its rooms. The organization spends tens of thousands of dollars bringing its team and treats the trip as an annual investment. A month beforehand, an internal “level-up summit” prepares people to make use of the event. During the conference, the team rents a suite, gets beer and pizza, and spends evenings hacking on things encountered during the day. Preparation gives the visit direction; evening work turns new material into something the team can try together.

The same attendee finds the leadership track more credible and less sales-oriented than in earlier years. swyx attributes much of the improvement to reputation: as AIE becomes established, more practitioners volunteer useful work. Some speakers were solicited, but much of the program arrived inbound. In this account, a stronger program partly follows from a better pool of people willing to contribute.

Topic tables could give those participants a place to contribute as well as listen. The attendee wants a participatory element within the event; swyx worries that an entirely attendee-led unconference would be harder to sell. A possible dedicated leadership retreat would create a setting for a weekend of unconference sessions and off-the-record conversations. Its timing and location remain speculative. The main conference retains its emphasis on well-produced public content and meeting the people who present it.

13

为奇装异服和不合常规的演讲留出空间

Could success replace the scrappy atmosphere with something more corporate? An attendee invokes the shift from hoodies to sport coats at other events. swyx counters with a playful “costume index”: two at the first World's Fair, zero the previous year, and roughly nine this time. Orcs, a T-Rex and a shark supply the examples. It is an informal sign of the atmosphere he wants—people comfortable doing things that might feel unprofessional at another conference.

Culture also has a concrete program mechanism. The call for proposals includes an option labeled “My talk is weird and doesn't fit.” That gives reviewers somewhere to consider work outside the predefined tracks, rather than making the taxonomy a reason to reject it. The aspiration is to establish expectations that survive the current organizer: serve business attendees while keeping space for unusual work and personal expression.

14

一份有力的申请能减少对演讲本身的疑虑

A team working on spatial reasoning for home builders submitted five speaker applications; the questioner was accepted for the remote track. The team has paying customers, a Series C and architects who became PhD machine-learning engineers. How can that work compete when the people doing it are neither Silicon Valley insiders nor famous on Twitter? The question distinguishes technical accomplishment from the evidence a conference committee uses to select a presentation.

The constraint is severe: 2,200 applications for about 300 spots, with invited speakers also occupying those spots. The vision track has only ten talks. Some go to proprietary-model work, some to recognizable companies that attract an audience, and some to less established speakers. Meaningful commercial work alone cannot secure a place when many applicants can demonstrate it. Frontier work, a distinctive proposal and a connection to a broader trend can help.

Selection also depends on whether the proposed session will work as a talk. swyx sits on the committees with trusted reviewers, usually past speakers, who vet and vote on applications. Applications are not blind, and prior recordings matter because reviewers watch them. A convincing presentation can give a reviewer a reason to advocate for someone unfamiliar: “We'll fight for you.”

The practical next step is to record talks at meetups or other conferences. A video does not need 1,000 views to demonstrate clear delivery and a useful conference session. That supplies evidence of speaking ability alongside the technical proposal. It improves the committee's ability to judge the application; it cannot remove the scarcity of slots or the acknowledged mistakes in the selection process.

15

海报把学术成果带给业界观众

The final substantive exchange concerns students and academics who gained access through a partner conference. An attendee values the energy and conceptual work of people early in their careers. This is AIE's first year running poster sessions. Although the event began partly in reaction to academic conference practices—especially submission deadlines that swyx considers too far ahead of a fast-moving field—it now wants to bring academia into industry, complementing the movement of industry into academic venues.

The initial poster selection was delegated entirely to the academic partner. AIE provided the venue and industry audience without building its own review process for this first edition. Both sides have invited the other back. A future peer-review process or journal is raised as a possibility, which would add a publication role to the event rather than simply expanding the poster area.

There is also a reason to preserve an informal presentation venue. swyx values the ability for researchers to present existing work again to an industry audience, without treating the appearance as a competing publication; whether a particular submission permits that still depends on its applicable exclusivity rules. Students gain exposure to people interested in hiring them. The World's Fair has the physical space to support those encounters, making its scale useful for a format beyond the main-stage talk.

要点总结

  • 工作坊入场需要尽快告诉参会者能否进去。录像保留了内容，却无法弥补在满员的房间外等上一整天。
  • 2027 年的计划是保持 World's Fair 的规模和场地不变，更好地利用现有空间，并通过在其他地点举办活动扩大参与范围。
  • 开放的日程数据让人可以做自定义界面，但默认体验仍需要可靠的导航、有用的筛选、清晰的位置和一致的已保存日程。
  • 过往的演讲录像有助于不熟悉的讲者证明演讲质量。技术价值要与有限的分会场容量、受邀讲者和观众吸引力竞争。
  • 首批海报环节借助合作伙伴的遴选流程，把学术成果和学生与业界参会者联系起来；增加正式发表会改变这一定位。
