招聘“能上岗”的智能体 + 创业擂台决赛

Howie Liu 讲解了让智能体持续工作、交接任务和请求审批的产品机制。随后三场创业路演检验了这些能力在哪里能形成生意——以及领域知识、共享上下文和受众局限在哪里依然重要。

01

从补全文本到选择接下来发生什么

Airtable began with an ambition investors considered implausibly broad: let people build many kinds of applications on one horizontal platform. Howie Liu, founder of Airtable and its HyperAgent product, brings that same ambition to agents. The product question is how to give many different businesses useful autonomous work without requiring each business to assemble all the underlying machinery.

His starting point is a progression in what the interface lets a user delegate. A completion model receives tokens and emits more tokens until it stops. Instruction tuning and conversational behavior make a different product possible: a chatbot that can respond to requests through a back-and-forth exchange. The underlying capability matters because it changes how people can express work, from supplying a pattern to having a conversation.

An agent adds a consequential choice: it can decide its next reasoning step or tool call as the task unfolds. Liu uses a definition aligned with Anthropic’s distinction between agents and workflows. A prescribed workflow determines the path in advance; an agent repeatedly uses the model to choose among possible actions. That freedom is what lets it handle an open-ended request, and what makes keeping it on task a product problem of its own.

02

心跳重启工作，编排拆分工作

A reactive agent can work for twenty minutes or an hour and still have the same basic lifecycle as a chat request: the user starts it, it does the task, and it finishes. Liu calls the next form factor a “claw,” borrowing from OpenClaw: an agent that can wake up again and continue pursuing a longer-running goal.

The always-on behavior comes from two mechanisms operating at different timescales:

- Within a wake-up: the model chooses actions, calls tools and receives their results. Each tool response invokes the model again, continuing the agent’s turns until it reaches a stopping point. - Between wake-ups: a heartbeat invokes the agent again after that run has stopped. The agent gets another opportunity to pursue its goal or find a way around a blocker.

A heartbeat therefore supplies repeated opportunities to act; the model still has to choose useful actions. Liu recalls BabyAGI and AutoGPT as experiments that could run for hours while drifting into unproductive rabbit holes. His assessment is that stronger models now sustain enough coherence to make longer jobs more useful. This is a capability judgment, rather than a claim that restarting an agent guarantees progress.

Agent orchestration adds a separate capability. One agent can distribute work to other agents, including parallel work of the kind Liu describes using in Claude Code. Breaking a large problem into differently scoped tasks gives each agent a more manageable task and context window. The organizational work then includes handing off results and coordinating the pieces. Longer runtime and more workers solve different problems: one extends opportunities to act; the other divides what each worker must understand.

03

一次后院咨询变成了一份勘测报告和方案

The concrete example is a landscaping business, presented as a realistic scenario based on HyperAgent customer usage. Its ambition extends across prospecting, quoting, pitching and managing the work. Agents coordinate the business processes surrounding a physical service; the example does not suggest that software performs the garden work itself. The surprising customer is a traditionally offline business applying a fleet of agents to its operating model.

The workflow starts with an inbound inquiry: a prospective customer supplies information about a messy backyard and asks for a quote and a proposal. A triage agent researches whether the person is legitimate, whether the lead is worthwhile and whether the project fits the business. It then hands the project to a surveyor agent, whose job is to inspect the landscape and develop a proposed redesign. The division separates a commercial qualification decision from the work of understanding and presenting the project.

What can the surveyor extract from the customer’s original images or video? Each HyperAgent thread has a sandbox virtual machine where it can write code and manipulate files. For video, the agent can use FFmpeg to extract individual frames and then analyze them. That gives the proposal process a way to inspect supplied material and assemble relevant information, rather than relying only on the customer’s written description. The resulting work includes proposed imagery and a client pitch deck.

The economic change is the amount of attention a small project can receive. Liu compares the bespoke presentation one might expect for a client spending more than $5 million with a small backyard job. Generating the survey, imagery and pitch makes that level of presentation more accessible to a small business. The example establishes the intended output and workflow, but supplies no measured proposal cost, survey accuracy or customer conversion rate.

04

先把承诺准备好，再去问对此负责的人

Producing a proposal does not give the agent permission to commit the business. A binding quote or agreement to perform work is a high-impact action, and the owner still controls the relevant policies and decisions. The useful intervention comes after the agent has prepared the work: the human receives something concrete to assess and can unblock the next action.

What does the owner review? The agent returns with a qualified lead, a surveyed project, a pitch deck, a proposal and a quote based on the project factors it considered. It asks for approval to send that package to the customer. Liu describes the proposal itself as an interactive webpage, including for a landscaping job around $10,000. The owner can review the prepared commercial offer before the business exposes it to the customer.

Where does information move, and where does permission enter? The handoff below separates the agents’ preparation from the owner’s authorization. The customer submission starts the work; the customer-facing offer remains behind a human decision.

Information prepares the offer; approval permits sending it Agent handoffs carry the project toward a prepared quote. The owner controls whether that quote goes to the customer. - Customer inquiry — Backyard information and supplied imagery or video. - Triage agent — Research legitimacy, lead quality and project fit. - Surveyor agent — Inspect supplied material and prepare the project proposal. - Business owner — Review the prepared proposal and quote; decide whether to approve sending. - Send offer to customer — A proposed next action requiring the owner's approval. - Customer inquiry → Triage agent (Information: inbound submission) - Triage agent → Surveyor agent (Work handoff: survey the project) - Surveyor agent → Business owner (Information: prepared proposal and quote) - Business owner → Send offer to customer (Control: only if approved)

05

记住纠正，让阻碍可见

Approval is also an opportunity to teach the agent something useful for its next job. Through Slack, email or another invocation method, the owner can correct the proposal’s format, ask for a different approach or point out a missed cost factor. A correction about an omitted cost matters beyond the current document: remembering it should change what the agent considers when preparing a later quote.

Liu’s proposed mechanism is accumulating memories and updating skills as interactions happen, without waiting for a subsequent model fine-tuning run. Here, “learning” includes changing the context and instructions available to the agent. The recording does not detail how those updates are stored, selected or checked, so it does not establish that every remembered correction reliably improves future work. The product requirement is clear: useful feedback should survive the conversation that produced it.

The interface for supervising this work must change too. Coding provides Liu’s comparison: manual editing gives way to Copilot-style completions, then conversational changes, then oversight of multiple agents working independently. Once several jobs can run at once—including overnight—the limiting human activity increasingly becomes resolving the decisions that stop those jobs.

An orchestration control plane should therefore show three kinds of information together: what agents are working on, what blocks them and where they are handing work to one another. Liu’s analogy is zooming out over SimCity. The operator needs enough of the whole landscape to intervene where it matters, without opening every task individually. That is the practical interface consequence of employing a fleet.

Liu closes by forecasting that this way of operating will become necessary across industries, and invites the audience to try HyperAgent. The offer announced at the event was $1,000 in inference credit. His larger claim remains a forecast: humans will increasingly orchestrate agents that can also orchestrate one another, and companies will compete on how effectively they put that capacity to work.

06

三位决赛选手把“智能体生意”的论点搬上了舞台

The Startup Battlefield turns that product thesis into three business proposals. HyperAgent’s Founding Five Hundred program backs companies trying to operate with unusually high agent leverage. Twenty companies were preselected from the five hundred founders, and three reached this final. Howie, Theo and Joshua judge the pitches and questions.

The contest offers $100,000 in prizes: $50,000 for the winner, $30,000 for the runner-up and $20,000 for the second runner-up. Its stated emphasis is an impressive, meaningful use of the technology and a founder worth knowing. That framing leaves room for very different products: a specialized transaction system, a broad collaboration tool and an agent team that builds businesses for creators.

07

大宗商品交易需要证据才能推进

The commodity-trading finalist starts with a coordination problem: transactions cross countries, currencies, banking laws and commodity-specific procedures, while evidence travels through fragmented documents and communication channels. Its proposed agentic execution layer uses a proprietary CFC state machine to govern specialized agents. The founders identify fraud and verification as central obstacles, and describe a first customer operating a Dubai-based trading company.

The pitch describes several pieces of the transaction machinery:

- Counterparty and document checks: agents screen both sides for identity, know-your-customer requirements and sanctions, while document forensics looks for fraud. - Deal coordination: a private deal room brings in shipping and logistics partners with live pricing. - Parallel specialist work: agents structure compliance, legal, currency, banking, shipping and settlement aspects of the deal concurrently.

The CFC state machine constrains that parallel work. Agents may advance only after clearing every applicable deny condition; the pitch describes evidence as a prerequisite for progression. Settlement has a further separation of responsibility: the system emits a settlement-eligibility signal, and the bank moves the money. It does not itself hold or transfer the funds. The promotional claim that thirty days can close in minutes is not accompanied by a transaction trace or timing protocol, so it should not be read as an established result for arbitrary trades.

The live presentation has a complication of its own: the judges cannot see the video on their confidence monitor, and an initial question goes to the wrong project. Once the Q&A gets back to commodity trading, the useful challenge is whether a sufficiently capable general model could absorb the product. The founders’ answer rests on access and procedure: agents pull from real databases, operate behind explicit gates and use sensitive industry information that is not simply available on the open internet.

That depth creates an implementation burden: each customer may need specialized information and integration. The proposed economics rely on large deals and per-deal charges. The founders cite transactions of $200 million or more and refinery activity of $9–12 billion a month. Asked how many customers would support a very large business, they estimate about 100, although the question combines a billion-dollar valuation with $100 million ARR, so the answer is not a precise revenue model.

Traders and banks are the intended entry point. The founders argue that stronger audit trails and immutable trade records could help restore bank participation and liquidity in a fraud-prone market. The judges push beyond whether the business is attractive today: what must change as models and competitors improve? The response combines creativity, aggressive shipping, product quality and distribution. The final roadmap answer—“Becoming the bank”—is an ambition beyond the current design in which an external bank moves the money.

08

Comment.io 省掉了人工传递上下文的环节

The next team compensates for the same monitor problem by describing its product aloud. Liu jokes, “Pretend like we’re a non-multimodal model.” Comment.io is a multiplayer Markdown editor where people and agents join a familiar document interface, notify one another and call other participants into the work.

Its motivating example is a Friday-afternoon outage. One person asks Claude what is happening, waits for ideas and relays them to colleagues. Those colleagues paste the ideas into their own conversations, get other suggestions and send them back for someone else to paste again. The people who own the incident become the transport layer between separate agent contexts. Each useful idea requires another manual handoff before another agent can use it.

Comment’s proposed change is a shared document where people and agents work with context in real time. Openness and pluggability are explicit product requirements, alongside an interface familiar to people who already work in documents. The presenter’s experience spans collaboration features in Microsoft Word and enterprise AI writing deployments at Textio. The intended users extend beyond developers to teams whose daily work already lives in documents.

Liu identifies the commercial contrast with the previous finalist. Commodity trading goes deep into one domain and seeks substantial value per customer. Comment aims to be a cheap, fast, broadly useful place that many agents touch. His suggestion that a product touching even 1–2% of agent token activity could become valuable is a hypothetical scale argument, not a reported usage figure.

Why build a new editor instead of using Google Docs or a GitHub repository of Markdown files? The team distinguishes moving a document through an integration from collaborating inside it. In its account, sending or reading a Google Doc through MCP does not supply the real-time collaboration it wants. The recording does not explain synchronization, concurrent-edit handling or agent permissions; the supported distinction is the interaction requirement, rather than a demonstrated comparison of collaboration engines.

The closing observation is practical: conference attendees repeatedly described building their own visual Markdown editor, then giving up and sharing an HTML document instead. That is the product gap Comment wants to own. A shared artifact must remain usable by agents while giving people a comfortable place to inspect and contribute to the work.

09

Built by Foundry 把对受众的了解变成了产品

Built by Foundry proposes an agent team in place of an agency for content creators. Its input is a creator’s handle. Agents watch the creator’s videos and read comments to identify a problem the audience would pay to solve, then build the business around that opportunity. The creator launches it, and Foundry continues working on revenue growth. The pitch reports that more than half a million people use products its agents built.

Founder Weston Bell-Geddes frames the business around two assets creators already possess: expertise that can shape a product and distribution that can help solve its cold start. The target is a recurring problem behind the content, rather than simply putting the content behind a paywall. In his account, creators limited to brand deals, affiliate links or $20 PDFs can use an agent team to build a recurring-revenue product while they keep producing content.

10

分发渠道有饱和点

Theo challenges the assumption that an existing audience can keep driving a creator-owned product. His Vercel example separates the audience into people who already use it and people who will not. Repeating the same pitch becomes less useful to both groups. Distribution can help a product start, yet repeated exposure to one offer can exhaust what that audience will do.

His own response was to move from four sponsors to 80. The value came from variety: different products could reach different parts of the audience, even when most offers were irrelevant to any one person. He accepts Foundry’s premise that creators bring taste, distribution and audience knowledge. His objection is that owning a company can add work and audience fatigue without solving the need for a broader range of useful things to discuss.

That leads to a different customer for the same research machinery. Businesses could use it to find creators whose problems match their target customer, study those creators’ streams and videos, and show how an existing product solves an observed problem. The agent’s ability to understand a creator would then improve product development and partnerships, while the creator could work with multiple businesses.

Bell-Geddes answers with a narrower example: Brandon, a homesteading creator with two million followers, loses track of the best settings on his freeze dryer. Foundry treats that recurring operational problem as a subscription opportunity, and the founder says users now pay monthly or yearly to solve it. The example makes the proposed product concrete: preserve useful knowledge for an activity the audience repeatedly performs.

The disagreement turns on the niche and the persistence of the problem. Theo’s technology audience faces many competing products and benefits from variety; Foundry’s answer is a specific recurring need within a different audience. Bell-Geddes says technology and perhaps comedy are harder categories, and reports that no creator has left Foundry because its creators remain profitable. Without the cohort size or observation period, that retention claim cannot settle the broader debate. The freeze-dryer example does, however, explain why a creator product can have continuing utility beyond the next promotional post.

11

Comment.io 赢得决赛

After a backstage deliberation and an event recap video, the judges return to announce the results:

- Grand prize — Comment.io: $50,000. - Runner-up — Built by Foundry: $30,000. - Second runner-up — the commodity-trading finalist: $20,000.

The winning team closes by thanking the audience for its feedback and inviting people to try Comment.io, described at the event as free. The final leaves three distinct product bets in view: evidence-gated execution for expensive transactions, shared context for people working with agents, and agent-built products shaped by an existing audience’s needs.

要点总结

  • 工具结果话轮让智能体在一次运行内持续工作；心跳则在它停下后给它再开一次运行。两种机制各自都无法保证有用的进展。
  • 园林绿化的例子把资质审核、勘测和方案准备，与业主批准面向客户的报价分开。
  • 有用的监督需要被记住的纠正，以及对当前工作、阻塞和交接的可见性。
  • 大宗商品的推介把专门的智能体放在证据关卡之后，把资金划转留给银行。
  • Comment.io 针对的是在彼此独立的智能体对话之间手动转述想法的问题，它把人和智能体放进一份共享文档里。
  • 创作者的分发渠道有助于产品起步，但持续的价值取决于受众反复出现的需求，以及重复推广的极限。
  • Beating RL With Reflection: GEPA and Optimize Anything —— 给出了一个具体机制，把执行轨迹和反馈转化为改进后的提示词、智能体程序和仓库技能——也就是 Liu 的讨论中只在高层提及的学习机制。
