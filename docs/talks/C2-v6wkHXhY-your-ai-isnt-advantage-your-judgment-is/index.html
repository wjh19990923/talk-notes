一家资产管理公司应该把最好的判断力花在哪里？

Sid Pednekar 跟随两个假想的研究团队，从财报电话会议记录一直走到投资组合决策，说明问题的选择、同事分享的知识，以及部署之后学到的东西，决定了 AI 的价值。

01

分析师省下了时间，公司却还没看到回报

Dr. Sid Pednekar approaches enterprise AI from asset management. He has spent much of his career in hedge funds and in investment and academic research, building specialized teams around three objectives: improving efficiency, driving profit and loss, and reducing costs. Those objectives give him different ways to assess the same piece of engineering. A tool can help an analyst finish research sooner. Whether the firm spends less to produce that research, or makes a better investment decision because it arrived sooner, requires a different kind of evidence.

The survey figures Pednekar cites illustrate the gap. About 37% of surveyed enterprises reported a positive contribution from AI to earnings before interest and taxes (EBIT). He puts the high-performing group at about 6% and associates high performance with a roughly 5% contribution to EBIT. In other studies earlier in the year, about 66% reported productivity gains, 40% reported cost reductions, and 20% reported revenue growth. These figures come from different studies. They describe the spread of reported outcomes without establishing how often a particular productivity gain translated into profit.

To examine the decisions behind those outcomes, Pednekar follows a research analyst at each of two hypothetical asset managers, Company A and Company B. Their workflows are thought experiments. He uses them to develop three practices that an individual, a function, or an entire firm can adopt: design the right problem, build shared capabilities, and compound the advantage.

02

更多的答案对判断力提出了更高的要求

Consider the different things a firm can now produce cheaply: software, analytics, recommendations, reports. Each can answer a question or enable an action. An abundance of recommendations still leaves someone deciding which deserve attention. Pednekar defines judgment as choosing what matters and how to act when several answers are plausible. Creativity helps someone identify possibilities worth exploring. Taste helps them recognize good work in a particular context. Weighing tradeoffs lets them choose a course of action.

Those decisions happen throughout a firm. A software engineer chooses what to build, a data scientist chooses what evidence to pursue, and a portfolio manager decides how much weight to give a finding. The C-suite and AI leaders exercise judgment, too, but much of the relevant knowledge sits with the people doing the work. Pednekar’s resource-allocation question is where their best judgment is worth spending. That question applies before anyone chooses a model, a harness, or a copilot.

03

在选择工具之前先追查那个迟到的信号

Company B starts with a copilot and looks through the analyst’s existing work for somewhere to put it. An earnings transcript arrives, the copilot summarizes it, and the analyst receives a shorter document sooner. Once the team discovers that use, it can feed the whole stream of incoming transcripts through it. The benefit is easy to observe. The cost is harder to see. Summarization reduces information, and a detail that disappears in compression may matter to the decision the analyst has to make.

Company A starts with a problem familiar to the people responsible for investments: material signals reach them too late. That wording leaves several explanations open. Perhaps analysts lack the capacity to work through incoming research. Perhaps the underlying data is poor, arrives late, or is unreliable. Perhaps the analyst already has the evidence and the delay occurs when someone must decide what to do with it. Faster reading helps with the first explanation. If the delay occurs after the recommendation is ready, the team needs to look further downstream.

Locating the bottleneck tells Company A where to direct engineering capacity and analyst attention. It also gives the team a way to assess a proposed improvement: follow the material signal far enough to see whether it reaches the decision-maker sooner. Pednekar connects this selectivity to studies in which leaders concentrated their attention on fewer, higher-quality decisions.

04

对研究流水线投入足够深，才能改变它

Pednekar recognizes the appeal of experimentation and says he has felt the pull of having too many opportunities himself. Company B runs into trouble when it starts asking how many AI pilots it can launch. It builds a document summarization bot, tries a copilot elsewhere, and adds a data retrieval bot. The firm can count each completed pilot and move on to the next initiative without ever asking whether material signals now reach the portfolio manager sooner.

Company A chooses the end-to-end research pipeline as the unit of investment. Engineering, analytics, AI capacity, and the people who understand the investment decision can all work on the same delay. If the bottleneck moves from reviewing evidence to acting on it, that second step still belongs to the project. Pednekar cites research finding that leaders direct more than 80% of their investment to high-value use cases, including transforming key business functions and creating new offerings.

05

给持续监控一个可以检验的投资论点

In Company B’s version, the work retains its familiar order. The analyst receives earnings transcripts and research reports, reviews them, updates a financial model, and sends a recommendation to the portfolio manager. AI can accelerate each handoff. Company A could change the analyst’s first action: ask them to define an investment thesis. Now the system has a proposition against which to evaluate incoming information, including evidence that could refute it.

With a thesis in place, monitoring and evidence gathering can run continuously. The analyst investigates exceptions, decides which developments warrant a deeper look, and weighs the evidence for and against the thesis. This gives a concrete destination to some of the time freed by automation. It also changes the analyst’s responsibility. Their judgment shapes what the research process looks for, as well as the recommendation that comes out of it.

Pednekar cites research suggesting that high performers redesign workflows more often. In his example, Company B still asks how quickly an analyst can send a recommendation to the portfolio manager. Company A gives the analyst a different job: decide what evidence would change the investment thesis and which exceptions deserve investigation.

06

让投资组合经理参与构建，并分享研究员学到的东西

Redesigning the research process takes people who understand both the systems and the investment decision. Engineers are moving closer to business decisions, while business professionals are building tools and automations themselves. Alongside forward-deployed engineers and product managers, Pednekar imagines a forward-deployed portfolio manager. Working with the builders, that manager can explain which evidence could change a position. An engineer working with the analyst can see where that evidence loses context. Pednekar connects this collaboration to a broader shift: he cites studies finding that more large enterprises see their technology teams as a source of business growth.

Pednekar describes the design principle as standardizing the core and personalizing the edges. For data scientists, a shared core could include datasets and their semantic layers, which give the data meaning. It should also carry the experience of using them. Which questions has a dataset answered well? Where are its gaps? What tradeoffs did a predecessor encounter, and which approaches failed? Those findings are part of the work a new scientist would otherwise have to repeat.

Scientists can pursue different research questions using that common foundation. An analyst working with a client may need a different workflow again. The practical test for what belongs in the core is what the next person should be able to take as a starting point.

07

跟随智能体从 Slack 对话走到工单

Much enterprise software was designed around one person, or a group of people, interacting with one application. An agent may need to gather context in one application, deliberate, and act in another. The quality of the data and the ability to reuse it become constraints on the whole operation. Pednekar reports that 71% of organizations in a study were modernizing core infrastructure to support AI. He also reports that 23% were investing between 6% and 10% of annual revenue in core system modernization.

He distinguishes three types of team agent, depending on what an organization is ready to support. An expert agent makes subject-matter knowledge accessible to colleagues. A workflow agent carries work across systems. His example is a Slack bot that opens and closes tickets: the conversation supplies context for a change to the state of a ticket elsewhere. A chief-of-staff agent takes a wider view, tracking the state of work across the organization and what still needs doing. Each depends on the firm making the relevant knowledge accessible at the scope of the job.

08

保留促成那次纠正的证据

Once the workflow is in use, an analyst may catch something it missed. Correcting a recommendation benefits its immediate recipient. Pednekar asks how the next analyst will benefit from the discovery. The correction might supply context that future investigations can draw on, or expose a need for a new tool. In either case, preserving the evidence alongside the lesson lets a colleague judge when it applies. A successful investigation offers the same opportunity to make an individual discovery reusable.

Company A can close that loop by returning to the problem it chose. If continuous monitoring frees analyst time, how much of that capacity goes into investigating exceptions? Do material signals reach portfolio managers soon enough to inform their decisions? These are questions the hypothetical firm would need to measure after deployment. Following them connects the original investment to the next redesign and gives the team a reason to keep learning after the pilot succeeds.

要点总结

  • 把一个重要的信号从来源一路追踪到做决策的人。在为更快的中间步骤投钱之前，先找到延迟出在哪里。
  • 用一个明确的投资论点指导持续监控，包括主动寻找可能推翻它的证据。
  • 分享数据集的含义、缺口和失败的方法，让下一位研究者可以从同事已经确立的结论出发。
  • 保留纠正背后的证据，并追踪腾出来的产能去了哪里。两者都有助于判断部署后该改变什么。
