深入 AI 经济：Stripe 的数据揭示了什么

Nilofer Rajpurkar 梳理了快速增长的 AI 公司如何构建产品、触达国际客户、把订阅与按量计费结合起来，并为“智能体作为买家”做好业务准备。

01

营收增长在加速——消费者支出也在跟进

AI revenue growth in Stripe's data rose from 120% in 2025 to 175% a year later. That second figure means revenue reached roughly 2.75 times its previous level. Nilofer Rajpurkar, a product lead working on Stripe's agent and developer experiences, uses that acceleration to introduce four patterns among top AI companies: faster building, international sales from the outset, changing pricing models, and earlier adoption of new ways to sell.

The company examples make the pace tangible. Rajpurkar cites Lovable reporting $100 million after eight months and $400 million eight months later; Cursor reaching a $1 billion run rate in two years, then doubling it in three months; and Anthropic growing from zero in January 2023 to $1 billion two years later, followed by a reported $30 billion. These are examples presented in the recording, with differing revenue descriptions, rather than a standardized comparison of company performance.

Stripe's consumer product Link supplies another view. The number of consumers adopting AI products rose from just under six million to over fourteen million in a year. Among top Link buyers, monthly spending on AI tools and services approached $360, up from $183 three months earlier. That spending figure describes the top buyers, rather than the typical consumer. Rajpurkar reads the pattern as AI becoming a critical utility: something people depend on enough to keep paying for.

02

更快的构建更快地迎来第一个付费客户

The first pattern begins upstream of revenue. In the charts Rajpurkar describes, iOS app releases were declining in late 2024, then grew at 24% month over month as agentic coding tools arrived. Delaware business incorporations showed a similar upward turn. The timing suggests a connection worth investigating; it does not by itself isolate how much growth the coding tools caused. More generally, the recording does not specify the sampling or controls behind Stripe's comparisons, so its figures describe observed patterns rather than guaranteed effects.

The opportunity extends beyond nontechnical founders building through hosted platforms. The share of technical founders also grew by 7% over a year in the data presented. For technical teams comfortable working locally with tools such as Cursor or Claude, the change is compression: work that once occupied a team for months can fit into a much shorter build cycle.

Does that speed reach monetization? Stripe's partner-platform cohorts provide the more useful test. Among some nontechnical users building with Lovable, Vercel, or Replit, each successive monthly cohort reached a paying customer faster. The latest cohorts went from a product to a paid customer in less than six weeks. Comparing cohorts makes the change visible across generations of builders, rather than relying on a single unusually fast launch.

03

Vibe Check：从一段业务描述到一次测试订阅

The Vibe Check demo shows what removing integration work looks like in practice. Inside Claude, Rajpurkar describes a new business and asks for subscriptions and invoicing support. Claude recommends Stripe. Adding Stripe's MCP server and agent skills supplies tools and integration guidance that help turn that broad recommendation into a specific implementation.

What business input changes the integration? Stripe's integration recommender MCP tool asks where the customers are located. Rajpurkar answers the US and EU, and Claude recommends Stripe Tax because the digital subscriptions sold in the EU require VAT calculation. It also recommends Stripe Checkout over card elements for its better subscriptions integration. The useful distinction is between choosing a payment provider and choosing a suitable combination of its products: customer geography informs the tax recommendation, while the subscription model informs the checkout recommendation.

Claude then provisions a Stripe sandbox through a new CLI command without requiring Stripe credentials upfront. It obtains sandbox API keys and, as described in the demo, stores them securely. This removes an interruption that would otherwise send the developer out of the coding environment to set up an account or retrieve credentials. The demonstrated environment is a sandbox; the completed payment flow is a test flow.

After consulting more agent skills, Claude asks permission to use MCP tools to build the product catalog. Rajpurkar agrees, then skips forward to the completed website. A subscription button opens a checkout form; entering test card details and clicking Subscribe processes a test payment. The observable result is an end-to-end customer subscription path available without leaving Claude. The recording skips part of the implementation, so it demonstrates the resulting flow without exposing every intervening code change.

The practical advice follows three parallel decisions:

- Prioritize developer productivity. Treat shorter build times as a business priority. Stripe's tools include integration guidance and guardrails for sensitive actions such as money movement, though the demo does not explain those guardrails' implementation. - Build, sell, and iterate concurrently. Customer acquisition should begin while the product is developing, rather than waiting for a long construction phase to finish. - Build differentiators; buy building blocks. Keep engineering effort on the parts that distinguish the product, while using existing services to enable them.

04

国际客户先于国际办公室到来

The older SaaS expansion story starts at home, then adds a country manager and an overseas office once the business is ready. Stripe's comparison puts the older model at 25 countries in year one and 50 by year three. Top AI companies instead sell to paying customers in 48 countries in year one and 120 by year three. The unit matters: these are countries with customers, rather than countries where a company has established an office.

Gamma, an AI-powered slide and website builder based in San Francisco, illustrates the shift. Rajpurkar cites more than $100 million in first-year revenue, with the majority coming from outside the United States. Across the top AI companies in Stripe's comparison, revenue from outside the home market reached 48%, up from 33% three years earlier. International demand has become a substantial part of the business model.

The geography includes both large established economies and emerging concentrations of AI spending. The United States, Japan, and Germany appear among high-spend countries; Brazil, India, and South Korea are highlighted as additional hotspots. Capturing that demand requires customers to be able to buy in familiar ways. Stripe observed almost 18% higher cross-border revenue among companies presenting prices in local currency, and greater than 7% conversion uplift among companies supporting at least one local payment method.

The Vibe Check tax recommendation is one piece of this broader localization work. Stripe's agent skills also guide agents toward local currency presentation and local payment methods. That embeds market-specific integration knowledge in the build process, reducing how much a developer must discover independently.

Global readiness therefore has three distinct jobs:

- Localize the purchase. Present prices and payment methods suited to the markets being served. - Automate tax collection. Account for obligations that vary with the customer and market. - Measure country-level performance. Track revenue and conversion by country so weak purchase flows can be found and improved.

05

订阅提供可预测性，按量计费捕捉不断增长的价值

Pricing changes when software does more of the work. In Rajpurkar's example, a company might spend $10,000 on Zendesk and then hire support representatives to handle tickets. Intercom's Fin instead manages and resolves support requests. That makes completed work a possible unit of value: resolutions, commits, or workflows completed. The pricing question shifts toward how to communicate and charge for those outputs.

Earlier software transitions also changed the purchase model. A relatively stable on-premises installation suited a one-time license; continuously updated cloud software suited a subscription. AI has no settled equivalent yet. Rajpurkar explicitly calls it “the early innings,” then turns to hybrid models emerging in Stripe's data.

How do the two pricing components divide the work? Replit began with flat subscriptions, then added credits as AI became central to its product. The subscription gives users predictability; usage pricing lets revenue scale as customers consume more. Rajpurkar cites Replit's aim of $1 billion ARR that year as an ambition, rather than an achieved result. She also reports that two in three Forbes AI 50 companies use some form of usage-based pricing, with many combining it with subscriptions.

Usage pricing creates its own product requirements:

- Use the customer's language. Developers may understand tokens; enterprise customers may think in seats. Choose units that help buyers understand the value they receive. - Show spending before the bill arrives. Real-time usage and spend visibility let customers understand accumulating costs. Surprise bills can undermine retention—the electric-bill joke lands because few people open one expecting a pleasant surprise. - Offer credits upfront. Exchanging money for product credits moves the purchase decision ahead of individual actions, letting users concentrate on what they accomplish during use.

Credits and spend visibility need to work together. Credits can reduce the mental accounting attached to every action, while visibility keeps consumption understandable before the next bill. Both belong in the usage experience, alongside the pricing page.

06

企业销售、渠道销售和智能体改变了产品被发现的方式

The next compression happens in distribution. The familiar progression—launch a self-serve product, attract startups organically, then add enterprise sales years later—is happening much sooner. Many companies in Rajpurkar's account begin enterprise sales during year one. Cursor started with a self-serve developer model in 2023, then quickly built a sales-led motion to land enterprise contracts.

Other sales routes are growing alongside direct sales. Customers buy Anthropic and OpenAI services through their cloud providers, while AI products introduce marketplaces through which other businesses can sell. Each route changes where a customer discovers a product and how the purchase happens.

Agents add another kind of buyer. Rajpurkar questions how much familiar human pricing psychology—$9.99 instead of $10, or good/better/best packaging—will matter to them. The more concrete signal is documentation traffic: agent traffic to Stripe Docs grew 10× in 2025. The chart described in the recording shows human and agent traffic converging. Her expectation that agents would read far more documentation than humans by the end of 2026 is a forecast, separate from that observed growth. Documentation is becoming a discovery route as well as an implementation aid.

07

一种新的销售模式改变了底层系统

Adding a sales route requires changes below the point of sale. A new channel can demand different product behavior, onboarding, pricing, and organizational support. An enterprise customer needs a different support arrangement from the long tail of self-serve users. Treating the change as a business-model decision makes those dependencies visible before a new sales team or marketplace exposes them.

Revenue infrastructure becomes especially important when years of expansion happen in months. Slowly growing companies could bolt on separate tools over time. Under compressed timelines, the seams between them become liabilities as customers move across sales routes. The proposed foundation is one representation of the customer, one product catalog, and unified data models across the services.

That foundation still needs explicit rules for movement: how a self-serve customer becomes an enterprise customer, and how pricing changes during the transition. For agents, the requirement reaches further. An agent should be able to discover, evaluate, and activate the product without a human in the loop. Discovery alone is insufficient if onboarding or activation still requires an exclusively human interaction.

The ending brings the four patterns back to the builder's choices: shorten the path to a working product, make international purchases possible early, connect pricing to value, and prepare enterprise sales sooner. Stripe's stated role is to supply the infrastructure that helps builders monetize and move faster. The Vibe Check demo gives that ambition a concrete scale: a business description becomes integration decisions, a sandbox, a catalog, and a test subscription flow inside the development environment.

要点总结

  • Stripe 的支付演示结合了针对具体业务的建议、智能体技能、MCP 工具和 CLI 沙箱配置，在 Claude 里跑通了一个测试订阅流程。
  • 国际需求需要本地化的购买支持：货币展示、支付方式、税费征收，以及按国家衡量转化率。
  • 混合定价把可预测的订阅与基于用量的价值获取结合起来；实时的支出可见性帮助客户理解由此产生的费用。
  • 更早介入企业销售和新的渠道，需要协调入职、定价、支持、客户记录和产品目录。智能体也需要一条从发现到激活的路径。
