多智能体：一个上下文窗口，许多孤岛

幻灯片原文
Greze frames multi-agent systems as a way to bring information from separate people and organizations into one context window. His ideal of access to all data runs up against privacy and security.

THE IDEAL STATE

Multi-agent = one context window, many silos

– The intuitive framing — "many agents each doing things" — is misleading
– Better: a multi-agent system is a way to populate ONE context window from many sources / silos — across people, companies, governments with the information that leads to the desired outcome
– Ideal world: one agent with access to ALL the world's data gets the right outcome

让 LLM 拿到正确数据的五种策略

幻灯片原文
Greze maps five ways to get siloed data into an LLM: bounded access, custom-tool privacy tradeoffs, shared silos, humans relaying information, and human approval of agent disclosures.

Five strategies to get the LLM the right data

BREAKING THROUGH SILOS

1. Give the LLM access to all the info (within a trust boundary)
2. Build custom tools with a power-to-privacy tradeoff you're OK with
3. Create shared silos that accumulate info over time
4. Use humans as the conduit across silos
5. Let agents work in a black box, then HITL-approve the disclosure

在信任边界内访问

幻灯片原文
Greze’s household agent accesses both spouses’ systems; an HR team could give an agent similar access. He argues this manual approach still relies on humans to organize data and does not improve as models get smarter.

STRATEGY 1

Approximate ”access to everything” within trust boundaries

– Personal: spouses' shared agent — email, calendar, Dropbox. Nothing to hide; runs the household better.
– Work: an HR team agent that can access everything the HR team can. Treat it like an employee; only HR interacts with it.
– This is the world IT / Security knows best — but it's manual and not dynamic (requires humans + de-silo-ification does not benefit from more intelligence)

If this is your only strategy, you lose hard over the next 2 years

保护隐私的跨孤岛工具

幻灯片原文
A proposed tool could search employees’ Gmail for useful connections and return a relationship score without exposing conversation contents. Another would let colleagues put draft emails in your inbox.

STRATEGY 2

Privacy-preserving cross-silo tools

– Build tools that share info across silos in a privacy-preserving way
– Use case: "Is anyone at my company connected to someone on the finance team at Acme Corp?"
– Relationship-strength tool: works across each employee's Gmail, return only a score (strength, recency, volume) — never conversation contents
– Another: a tool that lets anyone at your company put a draft in your inbox
– Pattern: preserve enough privacy to meet people's expectations
– This strategy and the previous one, trust boundaries, is what FDE armies at OpenAI / Google / Anthropic do — it maps cleanly to how SaaS works

Only this? You lose hard over the next 2 years

建立共享孤岛

幻灯片原文
A proposed AI sweeper distills information from private silos into shared workspaces, where agents can benefit from collective knowledge. The open question is what private information to share and who decides.

STRATEGY 3
Create shared silos

- Shared workspaces where data accumulates — within a company, or across companies
- Examples: Shared skills, encourage LLMs to accumulate knowledge in a shared mediums (your wiki, an Airtable, etc.)
- If everyone contributes and has tools and trajectories that emphasize getting data out, everyone's agents benefit and get better
- Best-in-class: a trusted "sweeper" AI inside each private silo that distills and pushes the right data up into the shared silo. This is LLM-wikis/brains but for teams.
- But how do we pick which private information is ok to share in a shared silo? Ask the user each time, or run an LLM-enforced policy?

Prediction: huge game-changer in the next 6 months — and it gets easier & better as models get smarter.

让人充当通道

幻灯片原文
Each data owner decides whether their agent can work on a request and share the result. The tradeoff: a request sent to 100 coworkers can interrupt everyone, even when few have useful information.

STRATEGY 4
Humans as the conduit

- Don't automate the flow — route it through the humans who own the data.
- Example: User A says "Can you ask all my co-workers' agents if are connected to someone in the finance team at ACME Corp?"
- The agent surfaces the request to each co-worker/s agent; each co-worker decides whether it's ok for their agent to work on the request. This is often what people think of as traditional A2A.
- The "no" is a feature, not a failure — the owner is the gatekeeper over their own agent (and all of the owners' data).
- Failure mode: the dumb version is spammy (ask 100 people up front). Nobody wants that.

“Draft an intro from my CEO to ABCCo's CEO (whom he met at AIE World's Fair), pitching our new product, cc me.”

黑盒计算与审批

幻灯片原文
Agents could search across private silos, then ask only the relevant data owners to approve disclosure. The approach depends on trusting the black box and getting the human approval step right.

STRATEGY 5 — BETTER A2A

Black-box compute, and only ask to approve the inputs if the output is worthwhile

– Let agents read broadly from silos in a black box, but stop for HITL before any write / disclosure — and only once there's a result worth disclosing
– Example: User A says "Can you ask all my co-workers' agents if are connected to someone in the finance team at ACME Corp?"
– Instead of asking everyone up front, ask only the few with strong relationship strength, figure out who does and then only ask those 5 out of 100 people" "Can I reply to JDG letting them know that you're connected to the CFO at ACME Corp and willing to do an intro?"
– The insight here is that if you trust the black box, you can do all of the work across silos and only ask the people whose data was used to compute the result whether they are ok sharing back the result.
– What A2A looks like: Agent A → asks → Agent B → does work → if result is good, HITL approval → replies → Agent A

网络效应

幻灯片原文
Greze frames A2A approaches as ways to bring information across silos into one context window. He sees privacy expectations and human review as limits, and asks whether agents can make privacy decisions across companies.

CLOSING

Network effects

– Trust boundaries · custom tools · shared silos · humans · black-box — all just strategies to get the right info into one context window across silos
– From our own usage, A2A makes systems extremely powerful, but it's still difficult for humans to wrap their head around what's going. Shared silos is the most straight forward model for people to understand. Black-box is super powerful, but it's tough to grasp.
– The main limiter is human expectations around privacy. And every HITL slows things down.
– As the economy lets humans do more with AI, agents get access to more data — including more public-ish datasets without these limits. Ultimately even privacy will be moderated by auto mode that's aware of individuals' and organizations' policies.
– Open question: all of these network effects within companies are happening now, what does it look like across companies and what are the first use cases for "auto-privacy/security" across companies?

Can we trust a future where we trust agents to make decisions around privacy?

智能体的下一个前沿：智能体之间的协作与网络效应

Jean-Denis Greze 把智能体协作重新定义为一个搜索问题：当所需信息属于别人时，如何让它到达下一次模型调用？五种方法把隐私决策放在了这个过程的不同节点上。

01

有用的单元是带着正确上下文的那次调用

People get work done with other people. Assistants should benefit from the same connections: another person’s agent may hold the information needed to complete your task. Jean-Denis Greze, introducing himself as Town’s CTO after seven years as CTO at Plaid, starts from that opportunity and immediately questions its framing. “Agent to agent” tells us who communicates. It does less to explain what makes the result useful.

The alternative framing is search. Immediately before a model returns an answer or calls a tool, its context window needs the information required for that decision. A more capable model can do more with that information, but the surrounding system still has to find it and bring it into context. Collaboration matters when it improves that process. 1:13

Greze sketches a progression from humans manually filling the context window, to retrieval-augmented generation using a search tool, to agentic search using multiple tools to explore available content. Each changes how information arrives. The engineering target remains the consequential model call: does it have the content needed to choose the right action?

02

那个什么都能看到的“不可能的智能体”

Imagine a single agent with access to everyone’s email, every company’s records, and every government’s information. In Greze’s thought experiment, it can assemble all the relevant context before answering. This gives multi-agent systems a comparison point: how closely can they approach the information available to that agent? The optimal outcome is an idealization within this model of the problem, rather than a demonstrated guarantee that complete access makes an LLM correct.

Even an infinite context window would not make this agent acceptable. People do not grant strangers continuous access to their inboxes. Privacy and security restrict what can enter the search process before context length becomes relevant. Greze invokes the Coase theorem as an analogy: removing information barriers and transaction costs can improve negotiation outcomes, while real-world constraints prevent the frictionless ideal. Here, the constraint is permission to use information.

The five approaches that follow place that permission decision in different places. Some restrict the searchable data in advance. Others permit broad internal search and control what leaves it. Their usefulness depends both on the information they recover and on how much human work they require as models improve.

03

限制访问，或者限制工具返回什么

The first strategy gives an agent broad access inside an existing trust boundary. An HR team agent might read the HR systems available to an employee, perhaps using the access level of the team’s most junior member. Everyone on the team can ask it questions. This fits a familiar SaaS security model, which helps explain its appeal to IT and security teams.

Its limitation is structural. Humans still choose the accessible data, and information outside that selection remains outside it. Better models may answer better within the permitted material, but they do not automatically dissolve the divisions between teams. Greze’s two tests are whether the system naturally needs fewer humans over time and whether model improvements expand what the approach can accomplish. A manually defined team silo fares poorly on both.

The second strategy builds tools that trade some information for a useful capability. Consider the question that returns throughout the talk: does anyone at the company know someone on Acme Corp’s finance team? Unrestricted search would read everyone’s Gmail, identify exchanges with Acme employees, inspect profiles, and find a connection to the CFO. That would also expose far more correspondence than the requester needs.

A narrower tool can accept a company domain and a target role, search the mail internally, and return ranked relationship-strength scores. The requesting agent receives a useful result without receiving the underlying emails. It then messages Bob on Slack about his connection to Jane, Acme’s CFO. Bob confirms the relationship and agrees to an introduction. The observable change is from a broad question about the company’s network to a specific, approved introduction; the tool supplies the discovery step, while Bob supplies consent for the next action.

- Relationship lookup: Broad mail access stays inside a purpose-built tool; the agent receives a limited relationship result and contacts the relevant colleague. - Draft placement: Colleagues can put draft emails in someone’s inbox, reducing the clicks needed when sales staff would otherwise ask that person to write an introduction.

Town uses this kind of tool for some recurring user activities, with an opt-out. More participants make more connections available, creating the proposed network effect. But privacy is still a product decision: people must understand and accept the information a tool reveals. Humans design each tool and explain its behavior, so the approach remains largely manual rather than expanding automatically with model capability.

04

把可共享的知识从私有孤岛中移出来

The third strategy creates a shared place where useful information accumulates. Private records can stay private while material suitable for the company or a subgroup moves into a wiki, database, or other common store. All relevant agents can search that store. The benefit comes from making already-shareable knowledge available beyond the conversation or inbox where it originated.

- Shared skills: An engineer saves a better database-profiling procedure in the repository. When another engineer encounters a slow query, their agent can use that procedure instead of rediscovering it. - Shared records: Agents contribute information to a wiki or Airtable over time. This requires both tools for reading and writing the store and instructions or incentives that make those actions part of ordinary work.

A sweeper AI makes contribution a separate, recurring job. An agent inside each private silo knows what must remain there and knows the available shared destinations. At the end of the day, it examines new information and moves suitable contributions into those spaces. “Public” here means available within the company, rather than published on the internet. 10:13

Who decides which contributions may leave? Two versions put the decision in different hands:

- Human approval: The model prepares a list of proposed contributions and asks the user to approve publication to the shared space. It removes much of the writing work while retaining a review step. - Policy enforcement: The model applies a policy itself and publishes the information it judges permissible. That removes routine approvals but makes disclosure quality depend on the model’s application of the policy.

What must the sweeper know to move information safely? The diagram separates the new private material from the rules and destination descriptions used to evaluate it. Both approval paths lead to the same shared store; they differ in who authorizes the contribution. This is where the shared-silo approach can begin to benefit from better judgment by the model.

Greze forecasts adoption of automatic policy enforcement within the next six months, especially in high-trust companies with ten or fifty employees and clear exclusions such as finance and HR data. He expects large enterprises to move more slowly. This is his adoption forecast, not evidence that the disclosure problem has already been solved.

How a sweeper contributes private knowledge A sweeper evaluates new material using retention rules and shared-space descriptions. A human or the model’s policy judgment authorizes what leaves the silo. - New private information — Material accumulated inside a private silo. - Policy and destinations — What must stay private, and descriptions of the shared spaces. - Daily sweeper — Examines new information and selects contributions. - Human approval — The user reviews proposed contributions. - Automatic policy decision — The LLM decides what the policy permits. - Company shared spaces — Contributions become available to other agents. - New private information → Daily sweeper (Examine new material) - Policy and destinations → Daily sweeper (Guide selection) - Daily sweeper → Human approval (Propose contributions) - Daily sweeper → Automatic policy decision (Apply policy) - Human approval → Company shared spaces (Publish approved material) - Automatic policy decision → Company shared spaces (Publish permitted material)

05

先搜索，再去问掌握相关信息的那个人

The fourth strategy keeps humans in the information path. Your agent asks another person’s agent about an Acme finance connection. That person approves searching their email, sees the result, and separately approves sending it back. Consent precedes both discovery and disclosure.

This becomes expensive when only a few people hold the answer. In the hypothetical hundred-person company, the question can trigger a hundred Slack requests for permission. Most people must respond simply to establish that their information is irrelevant. Human attention becomes part of the search cost.

The fifth strategy, the black box, changes the timing. A trusted LLM can search across private silos without asking each owner first, while its trace remains inaccessible to ordinary participants. It stops at the answer-sharing point or before a write tool call, identifies the information needed for that output or action, and requests approval from the people who own it. Greze presents this as a powerful design he has rarely seen in practice.

Return to Bob and Jane. The request goes to all the company’s agents, which search Gmail and other private stores automatically. The black-box agent receives twenty matching connections, compares their strength using the email contacts, and selects Bob. Only then does Bob receive a prompt asking whether his connection to Jane may be shared with Jean-Denis for an introduction. The change from the previous approach is visible: discovery still spans a hundred people’s information, but the approval request reaches the selected owner.

Where does human attention enter this search? The diagram places Bob’s approval after candidate discovery and ranking, before disclosure. That saves irrelevant approval requests by giving the internal search much broader access. The privacy decision has moved later; the need to trust the search system has grown.

The final output must contain only information approved for sharing. Even a narrow relationship answer can expose something sensitive: asking about a connection to a recruiter at another company could reveal that a colleague is interviewing there. Hiding the search trace does not remove that inference. The approval step and the content allowed through it need careful design.

The Acme introduction through a black box In the hypothetical example, broad automatic search finds twenty candidates, ranking selects Bob, and Bob approves sharing his connection before disclosure. - Find an Acme CFO connection — Jean-Denis asks the company-wide question. - Search private silos — Company agents inspect Gmail and private information without individual search approvals. - Twenty connections — The black-box agent receives the matching candidates. - Select Bob — Compare connection strength using the email contacts. - Ask Bob — May his connection to Jane be shared for the introduction? - Approved disclosure — Share the approved relationship information with Jean-Denis. - Find an Acme CFO connection → Search private silos (Distribute request to agents) - Search private silos → Twenty connections (Return matching connections internally) - Twenty connections → Select Bob (Compare strength) - Select Bob → Ask Bob (Request selected owner’s consent) - Ask Bob → Approved disclosure (Proceed after approval)

06

共享记忆可能保留了错误的东西

Greze’s immediate-return bet is the automatically maintained information base, whether implemented as a wiki or database. Its value is straightforward: information becomes available for later work without someone repeatedly copying it between private conversations and shared records. But the same persistence that makes it useful can keep errors alive.

His personal wiki still calls his agent Apex, although he renamed it Ivy a month earlier. The old name survives somewhere in the memory setup and keeps resurfacing. A wrong agent name is funny; a wrong business fact carried forward into future decisions is harder to dismiss. The example illustrates stale information persisting, without identifying the particular memory mechanism responsible.

- Prompt injection: Material planted in a more open silo can enter context through agentic search and influence subsequent behavior. - Persistent factual errors: A model mistake can enter the shared wiki and continue supplying wrong information to later work. - Wrong disclosures: Automatic decisions can release information inappropriately, with consequences ranging from negligible harm to firing or a customer lawsuit.

The black box also creates an operational tension. Someone will eventually need to audit what happened. Greze asks who approves what, what gets logged, and what can be reversed. A system whose traces are hidden from requesters still needs an accountable way to inspect its data use, potentially through someone in the CISO organization. Secrecy from ordinary users does not eliminate the need for internal oversight.

07

从一个低敏感区域开始

The proposed frontier is automatic judgment about when permission is necessary. Greze compares it to coding workflows that moved from approving every action, through “YOLO” execution, toward an auto mode that intervenes selectively. For information sharing, the corresponding design separates low-sensitivity material that may move automatically from material that needs human review or always requires approval.

Supplier meeting notes make the decision concrete. A user asks for notes from a weekly recurring call with a supplier and its finance team. In the proposed system, the model examines the notes, considers the requester’s role, and decides whether disclosure is acceptable without asking everyone involved. The presence of finance participants alone does not settle the decision; content and role inform the risk judgment.

This design gives model improvements somewhere useful to go. Better judgment, clearer policies, and carefully designed tools could expand the set of sharing decisions handled automatically, including decisions about when to involve a human. Greze’s recommendation is to define a low-sensitivity zone where the organization already accepts model discretion, then develop from there. Expansion remains conditional on those improvements; a more capable model does not itself grant permission to disclose more.

The desired outcome is less human coordination work as the system improves. Greze gives that ambition a pleasantly concrete destination: a beach in Hawaii with his kids. The architectural question underneath it is whether better models can take over more of the information-routing work, rather than leave humans maintaining the same silos and approving the same requests.

08

更难的网络效应跨越了公司边界

Within a company, shared governance offers a starting point for agreement about access and disclosure. The larger opportunity is getting several companies to accept a common agent working across their private information. That requires a use case valuable enough for each participant to permit some cooperation while retaining restrictions on what may be accessed or shared.

Greze describes an unnamed finance company exploring this direction with investment banks. The potential benefit is sharing private information about private companies for activities such as lending, with agents helping decide what can be accessed. The example is exploratory and unnamed, so it supports a possible beachhead rather than a proven deployment. Its appeal is that another institution’s participation could make useful information available that no single participant holds alone.

The ending leaves a real question open: how comfortable should people become with agents deciding privacy? Greze expects more decisions to move in that direction, yet does not claim to trust a future in which agents make all of them. The opportunity and the hesitation belong together. Work across silos can become more useful as judgment improves, while every new connection still needs an acceptable answer to who may learn what.

要点总结

  • 评估智能体协作，要看它是否把正确的信息带进了那次有后果的模型调用。即使上下文容量无限，隐私也会限制这种搜索。
  • 自定义工具可以广泛搜索，同时只返回有限的结果，比如关系分数。它们在隐私上的取舍仍需要有意识的设计和用户的认可。
  • 清扫智能体利用保留策略和目标位置描述，把私有工作变成拟议的共享知识。人工审批和自动策略执行提供了不同程度的披露控制。
  • 在请求同意之前先搜索，可以免去无关所有者的审批提示，但这需要信任宽泛的内部访问，并控制最终的披露。
  • 从一个公认低敏感度的区域开始自动共享。随着这个区域扩大，持久的错误、提示词注入、披露失误和审计需求仍是设计的一部分。
