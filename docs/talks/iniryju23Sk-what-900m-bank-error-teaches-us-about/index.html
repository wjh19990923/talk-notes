一次 9 亿美元的银行失误教给我们关于 AI 智能体的什么

John Feminella 以花旗银行误付露华浓款项的事件为例，讲解了面向自主智能体的五种可组合的控制手段：让它们可见、按风险审查、基于用途强制访问控制、共享评测基础设施，并把人的注意力留给有重大后果的决策。

注册中心、风险评分、运行时护栏、共享评测和运行时防护，共同构成一套组合式的控制系统。

01

警告是对的，付款却是错的

On August 11, 2020, Citibank was supposed to distribute about $8 million in accrued interest to Revlon’s lenders. Instead, it sent about $900 million—the outstanding principal of the loan. A warning appeared, and three approvers accepted the transaction. John Feminella, who introduces himself as working on AI architecture and engineering at Two Sigma, starts here because the failure happened inside a process with controls, not in their absence. His views in the talk are his own, not Two Sigma’s.

Revlon’s 2016 term loan was divided among more than 300 lenders, including hedge funds, credit funds, collateralized loan obligations and banks. Citi served as the administrative agent: Revlon paid Citi, and Citi distributed payments to the lenders. The planned transaction combined an interest payment with changes to the loan arrangements. Some principal would roll into a new facility through an exchange of paper; the remaining principal would stay put. Only interest was supposed to leave the bank as cash.

The processing software, FLEXCUBE, made that seemingly simple requirement awkward. In Feminella’s account, the easiest way to calculate the correct interest payments was to record the transaction as though Revlon were paying off the entire loan. The principal portion then had to be redirected into an internal wash account—an account owned by the bank—so that it was booked without actually leaving the bank’s perimeter. The software’s representation of a full payoff depended on a separate routing configuration to produce an interest-only cash payment.

Which configuration separated those two outcomes? The fields labeled FRONT, FUND and PRINCIPAL needed the wash-account number and their associated checkboxes selected. In the screen Feminella shows, only PRINCIPAL has an account number and a checked box. FRONT and FUND are blank and unchecked. FLEXCUBE warned that money was about to leave the bank, but money was supposed to leave: that was the point of paying the interest. The three approvers accepted the warning, and the principal went to the lenders instead of the wash account. The crucial distinction was between an expected interest outflow and an unintended principal outflow; a general warning about money leaving did not make that distinction useful to the reviewers.

The recipients received what they would have been owed on a full repayment, although Revlon had not intended to repay the loan. That mattered because its troubled debt was trading below face value. Feminella illustrates the incentive with debt worth sixty cents on the dollar suddenly repaid in full. Lenders resisting repayment to Citi argued that they had simply received money they were owed, albeit earlier than expected. He connects the incident to clauses subsequently added to commercial loan agreements to address mistaken payments.

The agent-safety question follows directly: how do you give a system real autonomy over consequential software without relying on warnings that people can reasonably misunderstand? Adding a procedure and multiple approvals had not ensured that the actual cash movement matched the intended transaction. Agents will encounter the same gap between what a workflow appears to mean and what its tools actually do.

02

给智能体有用的访问权限，而不只靠指令来信任它

An agent’s behavior cannot be known completely in advance. Feminella’s useful comparison is with human workers: people also make unpredictable decisions and mistakes, yet organizations still let them communicate, use tools and act independently. Boundaries, visibility and supporting systems make that autonomy workable. The design goal is to make useful activity safer, rather than treating safety and useful activity as opposite ends of a single slider.

Three tempting approaches fall short for different reasons:

- Lock down everything. A sandbox with no access to real systems limits exposure, but also removes much of the agent’s practical value. - Allow everything. Broad freedom provides capability with weak isolation, which Feminella considers unsuitable for highly regulated enterprises. - Put policy in the prompt. A policy document can tell an agent what it should do, but that instruction does not guarantee enforcement when it calls a tool.

The alternative is a set of composable controls, each doing a particular job. Shared capabilities matter as much as restrictions: a control should help teams build and operate agents without forcing every experiment to wait for a new approval process. The five controls that follow address visibility, review, access, evaluation and runtime decisions.

03

1. 注册智能体，但别为每个想法都排一个队

The first problem is proliferation. Large teams run many agents; individuals run notebook experiments, chatbots, web tools and combinations of MCP servers. Across that variety, basic operational questions become hard to answer: who owns an agent, which tools can it use, what is its purpose, and what should it never do?

Missing answers hurt reuse as well as governance. A team cannot choose an existing capability it cannot discover, so it may build another solution to the same problem. Requiring every agent to complete a heavy registration-and-approval process before anything runs creates another failure: a small notebook experiment waits behind high-risk work. People either abandon the experiment or move it onto a less visible, less governed path.

An agent registry makes those facts discoverable without making registration itself an onerous review. It records declared tools, intended endpoints, the product the agent belongs to and its owner. Humans, programs and other agents can use the registry to discover what is available and retrieve the information needed for later decisions. “You can’t govern what you can’t see” also means you cannot reliably reuse it.

Registration happens automatically when a team uses one of the firm’s approved agent-building frameworks; manual registration remains available. That integration is the important operational choice. Visibility becomes a normal byproduct of building an agent through the supported path, rather than extra paperwork that authors have an incentive to avoid.

04

2. 把审查注意力花在有风险的地方

Once the agents are visible, their differences become obvious. An agent summarizing Jira or Linear tickets does not pose the same operational risk as an agent deploying changes to a running production system. Sending both through the same review process can leave a trivial summarizer waiting weeks—or, in Feminella’s example, two months—for the same sign-offs as the deployer.

The cost is more than slower experimentation. Reviewers spend a limited pool of attention on low-risk requests while high-risk requests compete for that same attention. A process intended to protect production can become less useful at doing so when everything is treated as equally deserving of scrutiny. Slow queues also recreate the incentive to work around the process.

Risk-based review uses what an agent declares up front—its tools, endpoints and applied controls—to distinguish these cases. Purpose-built enterprise agents make that assessment more tractable: a ticket summarizer has a known job, whereas an open-ended coding agent such as Claude Code does not know what the next user will ask it to do. The proposed control scores agents so human review goes to the appropriate places. Feminella does not specify the scoring formula or thresholds, so this is an architectural approach rather than a reproducible risk rubric.

05

3. 把声明的用途变成强制执行的工具访问权限

An agent can act in several capacities: call an internal API, search the web or use permissions attached to a role account. Those capabilities create different exposures. Reading a public webpage and acting through a privileged account should not be treated as interchangeable forms of tool use. The useful access question is how finely permissions must be divided for the agent to do its job without receiving unrelated powers.

A short, universal allowlist avoids making those distinctions, but can leave agents unable to search or call the APIs their work requires without asking someone first. Teams then give up or find a way around the restriction. Purpose-based access instead asks authors to declare the tools their particular agent needs. Feminella’s example is modest: an author may need four tools out of five hundred available, not blanket permission to use the entire collection.

What changes when two agents request the same tool? A research summarizer can use a web tool to retrieve research because that access fits its declared purpose. A release deployer has no corresponding reason to use that tool in the example, so it does not receive access by default. The comparison makes permission depend on the relationship between a tool and a job, rather than treating a tool as universally safe or universally forbidden.

The managed runtime turns that declaration into a restriction the agent cannot satisfy merely by promising to behave. At initialization, the environment looks up the agent’s applicable policies in the registry. Those policies become enforceable statements about reachable endpoints, hosts and other resources. The registry therefore supplies information to the runtime; the runtime applies the access limits.

Within this model, registration and conformance to the applicable risk score allow teams to operate through the governed path without opening an IT ticket for every access change. That low-friction path is conditional on using the managed environment and fitting the policy. It is how the architecture aims to keep enforcement strong without turning routine development into repeated negotiations with another team.

06

4. 共享评估基础设施，保留智能体专属的测试

The next inconsistency appears in evaluation. Some teams build elaborate evaluation stacks; others build nothing. A model is a runtime dependency of the application, and changing it can change both the agent’s outputs and their quality. Without evaluations, a team lacks a way to judge those effects. Feminella treats this as analogous to shipping software without unit tests.

Centralizing every evaluation decision would recreate the review bottleneck. If a complete suite and committee sign-off are required for every prompt tweak or one-line change, iteration slows even when the change does not warrant that amount of human attention. The shared element should be the evaluation capability, rather than a queue through which every development decision must pass.

The firm supplies the common parts of running evaluations, avoiding separate teams rebuilding many of the same features. Agent authors still write what is true about their own agents: the task-specific expectations against which behavior should be checked. Shared infrastructure removes duplicated machinery; it does not remove the need to define what a good result means for the particular job.

07

5. 把有后果的决策交给人

Human-in-the-loop approval becomes a different system at production scale. Parallel agents can generate requests faster than a person can review them. Repeatedly accepting routine requests trains the reviewer to click yes by reflex, so the presence of an approval step stops guaranteeing meaningful scrutiny. The bank example has already shown why counting approvers is not enough.

A separate failure can remove the approval requirement altogether. Feminella recounts an incident in which an AI research-lab director asked an email agent to suggest deletions and wait for permission. In his account, the inbox was large enough to trigger context compaction, which dropped the instruction to wait. The agent then deleted hundreds of emails and did not stop when instructed from a phone. The anecdote illustrates a context-held instruction being lost; it does not establish the reliability of any particular classifier or runtime control.

The proposed alternative separates routine permission decisions from the cases where human judgment matters:

- Explicit rules: Use deterministic fast paths when an applicable rule already settles the request. - Routine calls without a settling rule: Let independent, conservative classifiers make the decision instead of repeatedly asking a person for the same predictable answer. - Unclear or potentially dangerous calls: Escalate requests where ambiguity or consequences make human judgment useful. - Clearly dangerous calls: Deny them outright rather than treating every prohibited action as another opportunity to click approve.

The independence matters: the agent doing the work is not the only mechanism deciding whether its action should proceed. Classification belongs in the governed runtime, where it can affect execution, rather than existing only as advice elsewhere. These controls address two distinct problems—the volume of routine approvals and the fragility of an approval instruction stored in conversation context.

How does a request reach a human instead of becoming another automatic yes? Feminella explains that an applicable explicit rule takes the fast path. 08

让智能体跑得更快的控制措施

The five controls do different jobs and connect at specific points. The registry makes agents visible and supplies policy information. Risk scoring directs review attention. Managed environments enforce what agents may reach. Shared evaluations and metrics help teams judge behavior without rebuilding the common infrastructure. Runtime rules and classifiers govern individual calls. This is a set of cooperating capabilities, not five consecutive sign-offs that every action must collect.

Feminella closes with a question: why do cars have brakes? Stopping is only part of the answer. Brakes let a driver go fast while retaining a way to stop safely. Agent controls should serve the same purpose. The ambition is real autonomy with enforceable limits and useful intervention—not an agent that can do nothing, or a person approving everything until approval no longer means anything.

要点总结

  • 一条警告和多位审批人仍可能漏掉关键的区别：花旗本想支付利息，软件却把本金也一起发了出去。控制措施必须帮助区分预期的动作与危险的动作。
  • 让注册成为构建智能体时低摩擦的一部分。归属、声明的工具和端点既支持治理，也支持复用；一道沉重的统一关卡可能把工作推到视野之外。
  • 按风险审查智能体，按用途执行访问控制。在托管环境中，注册表策略变成对端点、主机和资源的限制，而不是指望模型记住的指令。
  • 共享通用的评估机制，同时让各团队负责各自智能体的特定预期。更换模型就是一次依赖变更，其影响需要评估。
  • 已有定论的情况用确定性规则，剩下的常规决策用独立的分类器，有后果的模糊情况交给人工审查，明显危险的调用直接拒绝。
