发布 AI Engineer Network

在首届 AI Engineer 峰会上，Network 为大会应用加入了基于 LLM 的参会者匹配功能，以参会者资料中描述的问题作为建立联系的起点。

01

会场里一个正在形成的生态

Who has gathered to build the emerging field of AI engineering? The AI Engineer Summit 2023 opens with two days of talks from founders and engineers, with Swyx due to set the technical context. Benjamin Dunphy begins by looking around the room. AutoGPT supplies an immediate example of how quickly the ecosystem is changing: a few months after being an open-source project, it is the summit’s presenting sponsor. Dunphy calls it the fastest-growing open-source project in history, without specifying a growth metric or comparison period, and points ahead to announcements from Toran.

Supabase brings a different combination: playful social media and product launches, alongside databases whose ease and trustworthiness Dunphy associates with being open source. He says the company became a diamond sponsor despite its policy against event sponsorships, then asks Paul and Ant to let him know how the policy change works out.

Fixie makes the possibilities more concrete. Dunphy wants to ask its booth sidekick whether it can see what he is wearing and recommend wardrobe updates; he nicknames the sidekick Derek. The visual interaction remains hypothetical. If vision is not available yet, he offers Matt and Ben an introduction to Logan Kilpatrick for a possible Vision API preview. This is an October 2023 prospect: OpenAI’s public vision API announcement followed in November with GPT-4 Turbo image inputs through gpt-4-vision-preview.

02

从投资到对话

Dunphy credits Microsoft’s investment in OpenAI with helping commercialize the movement. Separately, a later Microsoft-hosted report describes $13 billion in cumulative funding across 2019–23; the contemporaneous partnership announcement described a multiyear, multibillion-dollar investment. For founders in the room, the immediate opportunity is a conversation with Microsoft for Startups representatives in the Carmel Room, past the elevators behind the audience. GitHub’s neighboring booth provides a destination for Copilot questions. These are introductions to teams, rather than a promise of startup funding.

Cloudflare gets a belated birthday greeting, with Dunphy joking that it will be going through some changes. Its booth is next to Microsoft in Carmel, across the elevators. He then recognizes Alessio Fanelli, the venture-capital half of the Latent Space Podcast, whom he describes as no less technical than Swyx. Perhaps the talks will draw Fanelli back into founding—though Dunphy suggests a software startup this time, leaving hardware to the experts.

03

已经有用的对话能变得更有用吗？

Dunphy describes the inaugural summit’s audience as 500 selected attendees. They are experienced software engineers experimenting with production AI, or founders building AI-enhanced or AI-native applications and companies. That shared experience matters when attendees meet: the person beside them is likely working through related practical questions. He also recognizes livestream viewers and points them toward the next day’s opening keynote for announcements about expanding participation in 2024.

Conferences already do some of the work of finding relevant people. They bring experts from around the world together around a narrow subject, making many conversations useful before anyone has arranged an introduction. The next question is whether that concentration of expertise can produce better connections still. Shared interests make a conversation relevant; a stated problem gives a connection a purpose.

04

一个个人资料问题变成了匹配的基础

The Network application starts with the practical information attendees need on their phones: the conference schedule, the full expo partner list, other event information, and recommended restaurants, bars, and cafés within walking distance. On top of that event companion, Dunphy announces AI-enhanced attendee matching.

The feature is called the Generative Matching Algorithm, or GMA. Dunphy gives the name a plain explanation: it uses LLMs to match attendees with the right people. The announced interaction is straightforward:

1. Enter the problem you want help solving in your Network profile. 2. Let GMA use that stated problem as the basis for matching. 3. Connect with people the system identifies as able to help.

The problem field is the substantive input described here. The announcement does not specify a model, prompt, ranking method, or evaluation of match quality.

Dunphy directs attendees to the conference’s Network download page. At launch, matching is limited to people attending this summit in person. That restriction defines the available pool for the announced feature, even though the app’s eventual ambitions extend beyond this event.

05

把应用和匹配算法开放给其他活动

The final announcement expands the scope: Dunphy announces that both the event app and the matching algorithm are being open sourced. The goal is a simple, powerful mobile experience for attendees at events around the world, with the matching component helping those attendees find one another.

The implementation credits separate the application, matching, and infrastructure responsibilities:

- Mobile app: Simon Sturmer, credited as lead engineer. - Generative Matching Algorithm: Suizette Steller. - Authentication: Descope. - Database and vectors: Supabase and pgvector.

Dunphy invites attendees to talk with the partners outside about pgvector. Its inclusion identifies part of the infrastructure, but the announcement does not explain how vectors participate in GMA’s matching decisions.

After thanking the developers and partners, Dunphy announces that the repository is available that day through the Network landing page and GitHub. The closing slide supplies the destination: aiDotEngineer/Network. The release invitation carries the idea beyond the summit: other events should be able to provide the mobile experience and help participants connect around the problems they bring.

延伸阅读

  • Network source repository — Source repository for the mobile event application and its attendee-matching project.
  • AI Engineer Summit 2023 — Historical event page with the program, speakers, workshops, venue, and presenting organizations.
  • pgvector — Postgres extension for storing vectors and performing similarity searches, with installation and SQL examples.
  • Using pgvector with Supabase — Current guide to enabling pgvector and storing embeddings in a Supabase database.
  • Descope authentication quickstart — Current starting guide for adding authentication with Descope Flows or SDKs.
  • OpenAI's November 2023 vision API announcement — DevDay announcement introducing GPT-4 Turbo image inputs through the Chat Completions API.
