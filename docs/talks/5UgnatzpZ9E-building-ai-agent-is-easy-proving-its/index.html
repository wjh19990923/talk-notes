NatWest 如何检验 Cora 能否安全地回答客户

一个房贷问题、111 段支付对话和 14,000 起投诉调查，展示了 NatWest 如何决定哪些请求可以交给智能体处理，以及该对评分抱有多大信心。

01

每天 10 万段对话规模下的一种新答案

Jonathan Osborne, a lead AI engineer at NatWest Group, could build an agent that answered banking questions. The harder engineering problem was deciding what evidence would justify letting it answer customers. NatWest, the UK’s second-largest retail bank, had roughly 19 million retail customers. Its digital assistant, Cora, was their primary point of contact and handled around 100,000 conversations a day. At that volume, even an infrequent mistake could affect someone making a decision about real money.

Most of Cora’s answers were scripted. If the team had anticipated a question, it could write and test the path to an answer. A customer whose question fell outside those paths might have to rephrase it or queue for a human. NatWest was working to bring generated answers and agents to approximately a third of Cora’s journeys. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) would draw on the bank’s knowledge bases. Agents could also take actions on a customer’s behalf, such as helping make a payment or change an address.

That combination widened what Cora could do and the consequences of getting it wrong. A plausible answer could cost a customer money, leave the bank answering difficult questions from its regulator and damage its reputation. To seek approval for the customer-facing service, the engineers first had to establish which of their own measurements they could trust.

02

一个房贷问题暴露了智能体失败的四种方式

“Can I overpay my mortgage without being charged?” Scripted Cora recognizes the word “mortgage” and offers choices such as applying, checking rates or going to the customer’s mortgage. The customer can select “none of the above,” but the system has made little progress. Asking the customer to phrase the question differently is another turn spent trying to reach the answer. Queuing for a bank employee is the likely outcome.

In Osborne’s agent example, Cora retrieves guidance and says the customer can overpay up to 10% of their balance in a year without an early repayment charge. It offers to check their deal. That is a much more direct response, but the order matters. The customer has already heard a specific allowance before Cora has established that it applies to their mortgage. Then the customer asks whether they can close the account instead. A change of subject is ordinary customer behavior, even if it breaks the sequence the engineer had in mind.

Osborne identified four failure modes. First, an agent can sound certain about a fabricated answer and even cite a fictitious source. Second, the input can become adversarial through prompt injection, with someone trying to talk the agent into doing something it should resist. Third, an answer can be accurate and within policy while the conversation goes in circles. A customer who still cannot get their question answered experiences a failure that a factuality check could miss.

Fourth, the same input can produce a different output on the next run. The Cora team had accumulated years of regression tests for a decision tree whose possible paths it could enumerate. An assertion about the response expected at a particular point worked for that system. With an agent choosing a course of action and generating its reply, passing that assertion on one run offers limited assurance about the next.

03

Cora 在启动模型之前先过滤已知事件

Every incoming conversation enters a routing funnel. The first gate is a contextual filter for events such as button clicks. If a customer has just selected a known option, the event itself conveys their choice. This filter handles about 20% of incoming traffic. The second gate applies deterministic preclassification rules to the customer’s utterance and accompanying metadata. When those rules identify a destination, Cora can route the request directly. Up through this point, routing requires no model inference.

The remaining utterances can enter a gated relevance and rephrasing step, useful for detailed questions that depend on several pieces of context. Next comes the intent classifier, a neural network over embeddings that predicts which backend should handle the utterance. This is a traditional machine-learning component. A final set of policy rules checks whether this particular customer should have access to the predicted backend. A classifier can correctly identify what someone wants to do while the access decision still has to be made.

Four operational requirements shaped the funnel. The policy gate enforces access control. For auditability, every routing decision carries a code so the bank can reproduce and account for the route it took. For resilience, the scripted service gives Cora a way to answer when a model is unavailable. For governance, the team keeps routing rules in versioned configuration. It can change a rule in response to changing conditions without redeploying the whole application.

04

路由准确率留下了关于答案的三个问题

Suppose the intent router is 94% accurate. That tells an engineer how often it picked the right destination. It says little about whether the eventual answer was grounded in a real source, whether the customer’s problem was resolved or whether Cora recognized when to bring in a human. Osborne contrasted a router that reports 100% confidence even on the 6% of requests it misroutes with one whose 85% confidence is well calibrated. The latter gives Cora a signal it can use when deciding whether to proceed or hand off.

NatWest evaluates each answer across three categories. Safety asks whether the response could harm the customer. Task completion asks whether it accomplished the job. Experience asks what it felt like to deal with Cora. Each can have its own monitoring and alerts. An invented mortgage term merits investigation into the source and the answer. Repeatedly returning accurate guidance to a customer who is trying to do something else calls for a different investigation. An overall quality score could send both incidents to an engineer looking for the same defect.

05

支付对话审查揭示了话题转换上 54 个点的差距

Before automating the scoring, NatWest ran a human validation exercise in June. Three people independently reviewed each of 111 conversations covering 37 payment journeys across four brands. The payment agent passed 83% overall. Split the conversations by what the customer did, however, and the result changes substantially. Happy-path scenarios passed 97% of the time. Edge cases passed 76%. When the customer changed topic, the pass rate fell to 43%.

The gap between following the expected path and changing the subject was 54 percentage points. The customer who asks about closing an account partway through discussing a mortgage illustrates the kind of digression at issue. Testing only whether an agent could carry a cooperative customer through a payment would have missed much of the difficulty. Human review also helped locate faults outside the model. The two biggest causes of failure in the exercise turned out to be engineering issues.

Independent scoring exposed another variable in the evaluation: how consistently people could apply each criterion. Reviewers agreed 95% of the time on bias and fairness. They agreed only 64% of the time on intent understanding, the judgment about whether the agent understood what the customer wanted. That complicates the instruction to make an automated judge match a human. On intent, choosing a different human as the reference could change whether the very same judgment counted as a match.

06

一个平均分 0.97 的评判者几乎分不清好坏用例

Cora Plus had yet to go live, so Osborne drew the production evidence for automated judging from a separate system. NatWest’s internal complaints agent, live since February, receives a complaint, uses tools to investigate and writes a summary for a complaint handler. The summary should help the handler understand why the customer complained and how the bank can help. Around 14,000 cases had scores from both a human validator and an LLM judge, allowing the team to compare the assessments case by case.

On faithfulness, the judge gave a mean score of 0.97, with scores packed near the top of the scale. Humans averaged 0.83 and used a much wider range. Osborne reported an average error against the humans of about 0.2, a number that might look tolerable in isolation. The correlation was almost zero. When handlers marked some summaries down and gave others high scores, the judge scarcely distinguished between them. An engineer using its score to find summaries worth investigating would have little to go on.

A model change exposed a second problem. The team kept the input and the agent’s output fixed and changed only the model doing the judging. In Osborne’s threshold comparison, the reported margin above the cutoff fell from 0.8 to 0.2. The work being assessed was identical, yet the bank’s apparent headroom had narrowed. The judge version therefore belongs in the configuration alongside the threshold. NatWest also moved the judge to a different model family from the agent being evaluated. Osborne said the different blind spots helped bring judge and human scores closer together.

07

追踪每段对话，每四小时抽样打分，并为提示词改动设置关卡

Cora’s evaluation pipeline captures activity from the agent and its guardrails using OpenTelemetry and OpenInference. It takes a 5% random sample, then also selects conversations that hand off to a human, fail, run particularly long or show signs of an angry customer. The two selection methods serve different purposes. The random sample includes ordinary interactions. The targeted selection gives engineers more chances to examine behaviors that would appear infrequently in that sample. The LLM judge scores the selected cases across the quality dimensions, and traces, scores and experiments go into Comet Opik.

The bank runs in an air-gapped environment without internet access. Evaluation reaches the judge over a private path through an internal GenAI gateway. NatWest consumes Comet, a SaaS product, as a signed partner app through AWS. The bank also has zero tolerance for personally identifiable information leaving its network, so much of the evaluation takes place with that information absent.

Trace capture, scoring and release decisions operate on different clocks. Cora records traces as events happen. Offline scoring runs every four hours. A change to a prompt or the context supplied to the agent triggers a comparison against a known-good baseline in CI/CD. A hard regression gate holds the merge until the team understands the effect. This matters even for a small text edit because its effect on the agent’s responses can be disproportionately large.

08

trace span 定位智能体故障，特征漂移预警分类器的退化

In a trace from the payment agent, each LangGraph node appears as its own span. If the conversation is slow or breaks at a particular step, the engineer can open that part of the trace and investigate what happened there. The conversation-level score identifies an interaction worth inspecting. The spans give the investigator a place to start inside the agent.

The intent classifier needs another instrument. Customers’ language evolves after training, so the distribution of inputs can shift even while the classifier stays fixed. NatWest monitors input feature drift alongside F1 calculated from human labels, with thresholds for triggering retraining. Osborne found feature drift particularly valuable because a shift in the inputs could show up before a decline in F1. The team uses Comet for both kinds of work, with Opik for generative AI and model performance monitoring for traditional ML. Engineers can observe both parts of a journey while measuring each according to how it fails.

09

影子路由让风险审核员在客户收到生成的回复之前就拿到生产证据

Model-risk colleagues wanted to know how Cora would handle actual customer language before approving a customer-facing release. Yet the team needed access to production requests to gather that evidence. Shadow deployment separated two decisions that would otherwise have had to happen together. The candidate classified live utterances while existing scripted Cora supplied the replies customers actually saw. Reviewers could inspect the real range of queries and where the classifier would send them. Whether a generated reply would resolve a customer’s problem remained a question for the next stage.

After shadow routing, the team planned to roll out customer-facing replies brand by brand, gradually increasing the percentage of traffic exposed within each brand. At each stage, performance indicators would help determine whether to expand further. Some decisions could be automated and others required manual approval. Feature flags gave operators three levels of intervention. They could isolate a single backend for one brand, isolate that backend globally or use the global flag to send every request back to scripted Cora. Each change could take effect within seconds.

The complaints team collected feedback at a point where a colleague had a specific judgment to offer. A handler who wanted the agent to rerun an investigation first had to explain why the original attempt needed another pass. Capturing that reason before the rerun helped build the set of roughly 14,000 colleague labels. Osborne said other projects struggled to collect even a tenth as many. Those labels made it possible to discover how generously the production judge was grading the work.

The two projects exchanged what they learned. Cora incorporated judge calibration and regression checks after the complaints team found the scoring problem. The complaints team, in turn, planned to adopt Cora’s Opik-based production monitoring. Each agent addressed a different banking task, but the machinery for recording what it did, comparing assessments and detecting regressions could be reused. At the time Osborne spoke, the complaints agent had supplied the live evidence. Cora Plus was due to face its own customer rollout the following month.

要点总结

  • 对按钮点击和可识别的请求做确定性路由。对于需要分类器的请求，在分类器预测出后端之后再检查客户的权限。
  • 按场景和按评分标准分别报告结果。NatWest 83% 的整体支付通过率中，话题切换的通过率只有 43%，而且审核员在意图上的一致性远低于在公平性上的一致性。
  • 在逐个案例的人工与模型配对评分上校准评判者。每当评判模型变化时，检查离散程度、相关性以及高于阈值的余量。
  • 用影子分类在真实请求上检查路由，再按品牌和百分比逐步扩大客户覆盖面，并保留一条经过测试、可退回脚本化服务的路径。
