具备基础 Python、虚拟环境和 API 凭据知识，有助于完成 notebook 练习；文中会在构建过程中介绍检索流水线。

01

快速推理能让你构建什么？

What does it take to serve Llama 3 at more than 1,000 tokens per second—and then use that endpoint in an application? That is the starting question for this workshop. Rachelle Mattern, SambaNova’s director of solutions engineering, introduces the platform; principal AI engineer Petro Milan leads the practical exercises, with senior principal AI solutions engineer Varun Krishna supporting the session. The sequence moves from hardware and model orchestration to a live Python build.

The hands-on work assumes a laptop with internet access, a Python environment, and permission to install packages with pip. During the workshop, Discord supplies files, endpoint configuration, and API keys. Those ingredients support two increasingly useful steps: making an inference request and building an application around it.

02

围绕企业模型整合技术栈

SambaNova was founded in 2017 out of Stanford. Mattern introduces founders Kunle, Chris, and Rodrigo through their experience in hardware, acquired startups, and AI companies including Snorkel and Together AI. The company builds its own chip, systems, and software; at the time of the workshop, it is on its fourth chip generation, supporting pretraining, fine-tuning, and inference. Mattern reports more than $1 billion in funding, naming BlackRock, Google Ventures, Intel, and GIC among its backers.

The intended customers include enterprises and governments pursuing differentiated applications and sovereign AI. Mattern describes a platform targeting trillion-plus-parameter scale, but the integration problem is more familiar: a team must otherwise decide which chip works with which compute system, operating system, and models. SambaNova’s full-stack approach packages those decisions together. It does not require competing with every tool at every layer; the purpose is to make the layers work together without each customer assembling the entire stack.

Samba-1 Turbo is the public demonstration of that stack’s inference performance. Mattern attributes the cross-provider Llama 3 speed comparisons to Artificial Analysis and presents the service as achieving record-setting throughput around 1,000 tokens per second. The presentation does not specify the prompt lengths, output lengths, or concurrency behind that comparison; the later live demonstration gives a more concrete view of a single interaction.

03

一个应用接口，多个专门模型

Large general-purpose services from OpenAI, Gemini, and Claude offer broad capabilities and straightforward integration. For an enterprise, however, much of the useful knowledge lives outside their training data: proprietary records accumulated in internal systems and data lakes. Mattern describes the resulting concerns as adaptation, privacy, model ownership, and costs that grow with per-token usage. Smaller models such as Llama 3 8B can be pretrained or fine-tuned for specific tasks, but maintaining many specialists creates its own orchestration and training costs.

| Approach | Useful property | Operational pressure | |---|---|---| | General-purpose service | Broad knowledge; easy integration | Proprietary-data adaptation, ownership, usage cost | | Specialized models | Task adaptation; control over models and data | Many models to train, serve, and coordinate | | Samba-1 composition | Specialists behind a shared interface | Routing, updates, and permissions must work together |

Samba-1 treats the collection of experts as a managed service. Its example specialists cover legal, HR, and coding tasks, with different applications and groups consuming different models.

Four controls make that shared interface useful:

- Secure endpoint: Applications integrate with one endpoint to reach the available experts. - Prompt routing: A router selects the expert best suited to an incoming prompt. - Independent update schedules: Experts can be fine-tuned and replaced at different cadences. Mattern’s examples are annual finance-policy updates and quarterly coding-model updates. - Model-level RBAC: Applications, individuals, and groups receive access to designated sets of models.

The endpoint simplifies integration, while routing, lifecycle management, and authorization preserve the distinctions between the models behind it.

04

选择专家，以及选择如何调用它们

The product offers a flexible composition, where the organization chooses its models, and a pre-composed Samba-1 catalog. Mattern describes the historical catalog as containing 92 expert models spanning languages, domains, and tasks. She identifies seven foundation architectures, naming Llama 2, Llama 3, Mistral, Falcon, and BLOOM, then also mentions multimodal capabilities from LLaVA, CLIP, and DePlot. Here, an expert means a separate model with useful capabilities, rather than an internal layer of a single neural network.

Mattern says SambaNova developed or co-developed 12 of those 92 experts. The examples include Thai, Japanese, and Hungarian language adaptation, a text-to-SQL model, and BLOOMChat, which she describes at the time as the second-largest open-source model. 05

把专家放在三级内存层次结构中

The hardware behind the composition is the SN40L Reconfigurable Dataflow Unit, or RDU. Its memory hierarchy has three tiers: on-chip SRAM, high-bandwidth memory, and higher-capacity DDR. The mechanism is to keep a large collection of models in memory and move the models needed for execution toward the faster tiers. Tightly connected tiers make model placement and movement part of the execution strategy.

Mattern quotes 4 GB of SRAM, 512 GB of high-bandwidth memory, and up to 6 TB of DDR. These figures should be read as a deployment-level description, not a consistent per-chip specification: the original SN40L paper specifies 520 MiB SRAM, 64 GiB HBM, and up to 1.5 TiB DDR per socket, with eight sockets per node. The spoken SRAM and HBM figures resemble node aggregates; the exact 6 TB configuration is not established.

The practical comparison is hosting a large model collection. Mattern uses 500 models as an illustration: a GPU deployment may distribute them across systems and direct requests to the appropriate system, while SambaNova aims to retain the collection within one underlying system using its DDR tier. She claims storage for up to five trillion parameters, but the talk does not establish the precision and deployment configuration needed to interpret that capacity. The useful architectural point is the separation between retaining many experts and bringing an active expert close to compute.

给模型它所期望的对话格式

The first call is deliberately simple:

``python answer = llm.invoke("What is the capital of France?") print(answer) ``

The model supplies the capital but also continues with material the user did not request. Milan then adds the Llama 3 instruction format, pointing to the Meta Llama 3 8B Instruct model card. The prompt must identify the beginning of the text, the user’s turn, and the position where the assistant should begin answering.

For the workshop’s raw-string invocation, the same question can be framed with the model’s special tokens:

``python question = "What is the capital of France?" prompt = ( "<|begin_of_text|>" "<|start_header_id|>user<|end_header_id|>\n\n" f"{question}<|eot_id|>" "<|start_header_id|>assistant<|end_header_id|>\n\n" ) answer = llm.invoke(prompt) print(answer) ``

<|eot_id|> closes the user turn, and the final assistant header cues the next response. In the demonstration, adding the format produces the intended answer. Prompt formatting is part of the model interface, not merely a stylistic instruction to be concise.

LangChain Expression Language, or LCEL, makes the same sequence reusable: prompt template → LLM → output parser. The country becomes an input rather than a string edited inside the prompt:

```python from langchain_core.output_parsers import StrOutputParser from langchain_core.prompts import PromptTemplate

prompt = PromptTemplate.from_template( "<|begin_of_text|>" "<|start_header_id|>user<|end_header_id|>\n\n" "What is the capital of {country}?<|eot_id|>" "<|start_header_id|>assistant<|end_header_id|>\n\n" ) chain = prompt | llm | StrOutputParser() print(chain.invoke({"country": "France"})) ```

The pipe composition preserves the model-specific formatting while allowing the caller to supply country at invocation time.

围绕端点构建检索流水线

The second exercise lives under the historical Workshop → AI Engineer 2024 → EKR RAG navigation and has notebook and Streamlit versions. Retrieval-augmented generation, or RAG, supplements an off-the-shelf LLM with information beyond its training data, including current documents, without retraining the model. It can reduce hallucinations in some contexts by giving the model relevant evidence to use when answering.

The pipeline separates ingestion from answering:

1. Load and parse documents. Choose a loader for the source format, such as PDF, text, or PowerPoint, and extract editable text. 2. Split the text. Divide documents into smaller chunks, choosing chunk size and overlap. 3. Embed and store chunks. E5-large-v2 maps each chunk to a numerical vector. ChromaDB stores vectors alongside content and metadata; Faiss is mentioned as an alternative. 4. Retrieve for a question. Embed the query into the same vector space and find nearby chunk vectors using a similarity metric. An optional reranker can reorder candidates and discard irrelevant chunks. 5. Generate with context. Give the LLM the question and selected chunks so it can produce a grounded response.

The execution boundary is explicit: third-party tools handle loading, splitting, and storage. Embeddings can run locally on a CPU or through SambaNova hardware, while the generation model runs on SambaNova. Milan proposes showing both embedding options; the recorded walkthrough proceeds with the RDU endpoint.
