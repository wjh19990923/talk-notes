设计语言

幻灯片原文
Eve says the site’s design reference updates automatically when she changes a design or creates a component. It calls for warm, quiet frosted glass and forbids cards.

Design language — landing page editor

Last edited by Claude · 1d

A living reference for the look and feel of the new landing page. It captures the shared visual language of the components we've built so far. This is primarily the floating editor (toolbar rail + Wallpaper / Text / Reading-column panels), plus the handful of in-page controls that borrow the same language (e.g. the greeting language picker), so everything we add next stays coherent.

Keep this file growing. When you build a new component, fold its decisions back in here rather than inventing a parallel style.

Feel in one line

Soft, warm, and quiet. Frosted-glass surfaces float over the page on generous rounded corners, with calm motion and no fidgety press animations. The chrome stays out of the way; the content (and the artwork behind it) is the star.

No cards — hard rule

Never render content in cards. Do not wrap text, drafts, lists, options, or any page content in a bordered/filled box (a "card": a rounded rectangle with a border and/or background tint used purely to fence off a chunk of content). This is a firm, non-negotiable preference, cards are disliked across the whole site.

Whitespace — vertical margin between blocks does the grouping.

Bach (1685–1750) thought in counterpoint, treating music as a mathematically ordered system of independent voices resolved into logical wholes, most explicitly in The Art of Fugue (BWV 1080) and The Musical Offering (BWV 1079). His work is dense with symbolic encoding, including the B-A-C-H motif and number symbolism, and he was a legendary keyboard improviser: on an unannounced 1720 visit, the aged organist Reincken reportedly told him, ‘I thought that this art was dead, but I see that it lives in you.’

想象力工程

Eve Bouffard 把积累下来的想法变成了一个个人网站，然后探讨智能体如何构建界面、保留设计意图，并满足那些微小而即时的需求。

01

当实现变得更容易，什么成为可能？

If a model can build almost anything you ask for, what should you ask it to build? Eve Bouffard opens with that problem, prompted by increasingly capable models and the return of Fable 5. Her prediction is that implementation will become easy enough for imagination to become the bottleneck: the valuable work will include stretching our sense of what is possible. Near-universal one-shot implementation is her expectation, not a capability established by the demonstrations that follow.

The opening illustration, created with ChatGPT, depicts the Library of Alexandria. Bouffard uses it to frame a larger ambition: consolidating what humanity knows while creating new knowledge. She approaches that ambition through a much smaller experiment, begun a week before the talk.

02

一周的想法变成一个网站

Bouffard leads design at Y Combinator. A former YC colleague, Lewis, had created a channel called Lewis Thoughts in the Conductor Slack, sharing ideas as they occurred to him while thinking through features and product improvements. She adopted the practice inside YC with Eve Thoughts, collecting a week of stream-of-consciousness posts.

Two days before the presentation, she asked Opus 4.8 to turn that accumulated material into a website. The result surprised her enough that she describes it as feeling like AGI—a personal reaction to seeing scattered thoughts become a coherent artifact.

Before opening the site, she explains why capturing thoughts mattered. Paul Graham is a model for the practice: his essays expose not just conclusions but how he thinks. Bouffard credits those essays with part of YC’s continuing appeal to applicants. Building in public already makes a process visible; thinking in public makes the reasoning available too. Her own experiment began with a deliberately narrower audience: a few highly trusted YC colleagues who could read her stream of consciousness.

03

一个从个人兴趣推断出来的界面

The resulting personal website organizes more than a biography. From the Slack material, the model picked up Bouffard’s love of art and supplied a customizable background. It also collected admired quotations, YC projects, media appearances, everyday tools and books that had changed her life. The input was accumulated thinking; the output gave that material navigable categories and, in the case of art, an interaction.

The books include French titles—Bouffard explains that she grew up speaking French—as well as books she wants to read. Among the quotations, she singles out Graham’s advice from How to Get Startup Ideas: live in the future and build what is missing. It becomes a practical prompt for the audience: what would you notice was absent if you already inhabited the future you expect?

She then recalls Steve Jobs’s bicycle-for-the-mind metaphor and Gary’s extension of it: capable models feel more like a rocket ship for the mind. The site also preserves trains of thought from the Slack posts. It gives an overflowing stream somewhere to go, then makes the captured material available to revisit.

04

把品味变成可调节的控件

The model also picked up her enthusiasm for the Paper design tool and Paper Shaders. On the site, she applies shader effects to artwork backgrounds, hides interface elements to expose the art, and adjusts the effect through controls. The selected frame shows a halftone treatment beside color swatches, style choices and sliders. That combination—an immediately visible result and parameters within reach—prompts her prediction that much of design’s future will involve sliders.

She can revert the effect and separately change the reading experience: dark mode, font and typeface settings, and text width. Language selection translates the website’s content. The customization therefore covers both the visual surroundings and the conditions under which someone reads.

05

给项目一份记忆

The next layer makes the website’s construction visible. Bouffard adds a dashboard describing its stack and an early design system intended to keep the interface consistent. She also experiments with Toolkit to track whether the webpage is complete. Claude produces a checklist that includes an Open Graph image: a reminder that generating the visible page is only part of finishing the artifact.

She then asks for voice.md. The model gathers writing it can find online, including her tweets, and distills it into a Markdown description of her tone and what distinguishes her writing. She asks for an equivalent document describing the website’s design language, and reports that it updates automatically when she changes the design or adds a component. These documents preserve two different kinds of intent: how the project should sound and how it should look.

A minimal Markdown structure for that design document can keep the rule and its application together:

```markdown # Design language

Current conventions

- Typography: - Reading width: - Color and contrast:

Component changes

- Component added or changed: - Convention it follows: - Convention introduced or revised: ```

The useful mechanism is the update alongside the component change. A document that stays current can carry design decisions into subsequent work.

06

探索心智的形状

After briefly mentioning personal skills she can invoke on demand, Bouffard moves to a second project: Shape of Minds. She says she began it at ten that morning in her favorite coffee shop, using Opus 4.8 before Fable was available. The question is what unusually fertile minds across history have in common—and where each differs sharply from the others.

The interface lets a reader hover over a person to reveal what distinguishes them within a dimension. Bach is the demonstration: his profile appears beside the map of labeled figures. Bouffard describes looking for the spikiest minds, meaning people whose characteristics stand out along particular dimensions. The interaction makes an individual profile available within the larger comparison.

She moves through thinking, obsessions, quirks and origins. Other dimensions cover routines, prolific output, workaholism, love, interests, worldview, religion, habits and knowledge. Changing the dimension provides different ways to explore the same collection of people.

To reduce the information overload, she asks Fable to generate highlights of commonalities and unusual traits. These were generated about twenty minutes before the presentation, she says, and she has not read them in advance. She encounters suggestions about arguing, childlessness, naps and eating little live with the audience. They are unreviewed generated observations, not established historical findings. The highlights are also public for others to explore.

07

可复用的图像和智能体可读的信息

Shape of Minds also carries a visual reference: Bouffard says its design celebrates the Fable 5 campaign. She asks Fable to extract butterflies from the campaign imagery and turn them into individual components she can reuse throughout the project. The operation takes a visual motif and makes it available as a set of building blocks.

Alongside the interface, she creates a Markdown glossary containing the information displayed in the project. She describes this as a more efficient way for an agent to read that information than inspecting the rendered page. The same project consequently has two representations: an exploratory interface for people and a text document for agents.

The supporting tools extend to her own work session. Knowing she had a deadline, she created a timer when she began at ten so she could see how much implementation time remained. This is her software-on-demand practice: when an immediate need occurs, ask an agent to make the tool. She then shows an AI-generated group portrait featuring figures including Leonardo da Vinci, Salvador Dalí, Marie Curie, Nikola Tesla and Einstein.

08

小小的烦恼变成个人软件

The next example starts with a small social intention: making colleagues smile through emoji reactions. Finding the right custom Slack emoji had been frustrating, so Bouffard built a tool for searching amusing reactions. She also shows a picker for regular iOS emoji, addressing the same difficulty of finding what she wants, with click-to-copy behavior. The scope is small because the need is specific.

She applies a similar pattern to learning. When a topic catches her attention, she asks a model to prepare a short report. Recent subjects include the ten principles of good design and Renaissance patronage. The reports are deliberately brief enough to digest on the way to work or while returning from an event, fitting learning into time she already has.

Cloud workspaces suggest the next extension of that habit. Bouffard anticipates using Conductor Cloud to start multiple agents while traveling from place to place. Here it remains an expected workflow, rather than a demonstrated mobile capability: the intention is to act on curiosity when it arises, without waiting to return to a desk.

09

让演进过程可见

The last artifact is a backlog of changes to the website. It includes the final Shape of Minds tweaks she says she was making roughly twenty-five minutes earlier, showing how the repository evolved over time. After sharing thoughts, interfaces and project documents, she also exposes the sequence of changes that produced the current result.

Bouffard ends by calling the work an experiment and inviting the audience to share how they are experimenting with models. The invitation leaves room for both useful personal tools and stranger explorations: the open question is what people will think to make as the effort required to make it falls.
