token 才是便宜的部分

Steve Jamison 用 Jellyfish 的工程数据说明，为什么大得多的 AI 账单可以与更低的“每个合并 pull request 的分摊成本”并存——以及为什么吞吐量、质量和工作规模也应该放进这道算术题里。

01

账单涨了 60 倍，合并的 PR 多了 43%

AI spending rose 60-fold over a year, while merged pull requests per engineer rose 43%. Steve Jamison, AI research and engineering leader at Jellyfish, opens with those two observations from the platform’s engineering workflow and AI telemetry data. They make an awkward budget conversation: the bill grew much faster than the output count, yet neither percentage tells you whether the additional spending was worthwhile.

The timing looks suggestive. AI spending increased in early 2026, around the point when throughput began a steady rise. Jamison interprets the pattern as evidence that agentic coding is helping developers produce more. These are observational comparisons, however: the reported association does not isolate how much of the throughput change AI caused.

That leaves two practical risks. A cap might stop waste, or it might prevent useful work. A higher allowance might unlock more output, or simply enlarge the invoice. Comparing bills with another company does little to resolve the choice because the bill alone says nothing about what either organization produced. The missing input is the largest engineering expense: developer salary.

02

把薪资和 AI 支出放进同一笔账

Jamison calls the combined expense the “intelligence costs” of a PR: what the organization pays its engineers plus what it pays for AI. The accounting question is how much of that combined expense falls on each merged change. For a consistent reporting period, the idea is:

\[ \text{Allocated cost per merged PR} = \frac{\text{Developer salary cost} + \text{AI cost}}{\text{Merged PRs}} \]

Adding AI raises the numerator. If merged output grows sufficiently, the larger denominator can still reduce the ratio.

The relative sizes matter. In the August 2026 data presented, AI accounted for about 2.5% of this combined cost, with salary accounting for the other 97.5%. A dramatic increase in a small expense can therefore be outweighed by a more modest improvement in how much work the much larger salary expense supports. That is the specific sense in which “tokens are the cheap part.”

Across the year, the reported combined cost per merged PR fell 26%, including the 60-fold increase in AI costs. Eighty-three percent of the companies studied showed a decline. The denominator counts merged PRs rather than created PRs: review time is included in the framing, and the changes have cleared some level of quality gates. More completed changes spread salary across more units of output. This is an allocation result; it does not by itself mean payroll fell or the organization realized equivalent cash savings.

03

重度用户获得了更大的吞吐量提升

The population average hides a wide range of usage. Jellyfish divided companies into quintiles—five groups—by token usage and compared their results. The lightest group used about 60 million tokens per developer per month, with AI making up roughly 0.3% of combined PR cost. The heaviest fifth used about 2.5 billion tokens per developer per month, with AI accounting for 8.5%. Even in that heavy group, salary remained most of the expense.

Did the heavier bill accompany enough additional output to improve the combined ratio? The reported year-over-year comparisons put the two outcomes together:

| Company group | Growth in merged PRs per developer | Decline in combined cost per merged PR | | --- | --- | --- | | Lower four spending quintiles | Around 40% | About 25% | | Heaviest spending quintile | 67% | 32% |

The heavy group’s larger throughput increase accompanied a larger reduction in allocated unit cost. The arithmetic explains how that can happen: spreading salary across more merged PRs had a larger effect than the additional AI expense.

Jamison reads this as a reason to keep investing in adoption. The comparison supports a narrower decision rule: evaluate a larger AI bill alongside the output and combined unit cost it accompanies. It does not establish that moving a particular company into the heaviest-spending group will reproduce that group’s results.

04

一条趋于平缓的曲线仍可能带来有用的回报

How quickly does the benefit shrink as usage rises? Jamison describes a chart with tokens used on the horizontal axis and cost per PR on the vertical axis. The curve drops steeply at first, then flattens: relatively small initial usage accompanies large unit-cost improvements, while further usage accompanies smaller improvements. The top 10% of company-months by token usage used about eight times the tokens of the median and had a 25% lower cost per PR. The curve makes the distinction visible: diminishing returns can still be positive returns.

There can eventually be a point where additional token expense outweighs the output benefit. Jamison’s assessment is that most companies in the observed range had not reached it. That supports watching the combined ratio as usage grows, rather than assuming either that every extra token pays or that a flattening curve means spending should stop.

05

把开发者与他们自己用得少的那几周相比

The next comparison changes the unit of analysis from companies to developer-weeks. Each developer’s weeks with heavy agent skill use are compared with that same developer’s weeks without skills. This holds the person constant rather than comparing two organizations with different teams and practices.

During the heavy skill-use weeks, token spending was about twice as high, merged PRs were 27% higher, and combined salary-plus-token cost per PR was about 18% lower. The same accounting pattern appears at this smaller scale: a higher AI expense accompanies enough additional output to lower allocated unit cost. Comparing a developer with themselves removes one source of difference, but weeks can still contain different work; this remains an observational comparison.

06

检查错误——以及被夸大的产出数量

A lower cost per PR is less useful if the organization ships more mistakes, or if developers merely divide the same work into more PRs. Jamison examines those objections separately because they challenge different parts of the result: quality challenges the usefulness of the output, while work size challenges what the output count represents.

Quality: look at reverts. A revert is a merged PR that undoes an earlier PR. The heaviest-spending fifth already had the lowest revert rate before most of the token growth. Revert rates then declined slightly across every group, but the changes were within the noise; Jamison explicitly declines to call this evidence of improvement. The useful finding is that this measure did not show a worrying increase in severe mistakes as throughput rose. Reverts remain a limited proxy, rather than a complete assessment of software quality.

Work size: look at lines per PR. Lines of code per PR increased rather than decreased, which runs against the explanation that throughput rose simply because changes became smaller. But more lines can mean several things. Jamison’s recognizable examples include verbose inline comments and files full of largely irrelevant tests. Generating more code can enlarge a change without adding equivalent value.

Cheaper creation can also change how developers package work. An edge case that once needed another ticket may now feel like “one, maybe two prompts.” A developer adds it to the current PR or combines related elements, leaving fewer PRs to manage but more material inside each review. More capable AI can also encourage more ambitious changes. Those are plausible explanations offered in the talk, rather than an isolated account of what drove the line-count increase. They expose a real tension: coding throughput can rise while review becomes more demanding.

07

对照交付成本来评判支出控制

The closing budget question returns to the opening numbers. A 60-fold spending increase and 43% more merged PRs describe inputs and output, but their percentages alone cannot settle the tradeoff. Adding salary and dividing by merged throughput supplies a common unit for comparison. In the reported population, that unit cost fell 26%.

This framing changes how to compare two kinds of investment:

- Reduce AI expense. Model routers, post-training a model and token limits are possible ways to control the AI portion of the numerator. Runaway jobs still deserve attention. - Increase useful throughput. AI enablement may help engineers merge more work, spreading the larger salary expense across more output.

The talk does not measure those interventions against one another. It supplies the question to ask before choosing: which effort is more likely to improve combined cost per merged PR?

The practical recommendation is to track the measures together each month: the AI bill, merged PR throughput, and combined human-plus-AI cost per merged PR. Put reverts and other quality measures beside them as a counterweight. A falling unit cost is useful only if the work remains worth delivering; PR counts and line counts alone cannot establish customer value or account for all later maintenance.

Jamison’s recommendation for the companies represented is to keep investing while those efficiencies continue. The memorable line is “The bill tells you what you spent.” Combined cost per PR adds what the invoice leaves out: how much merged output the engineering expense supported. Bring that ratio to budget season, then keep asking whether throughput, delivery cost and quality are moving together.

要点总结

  • 即使 AI 账单上升，“薪资加 AI”的每个合并 PR 的总成本仍可能下降，因为额外的产出把大得多的薪资开支分摊到了更多已合并的修改上。
  • 支出最高的那五分之一，吞吐量增长 67%，每个 PR 的成本下降 32%，而支出较低的同行约为 40% 和 25%。这些比较并不能单独分离出 AI 的因果贡献。
  • 回滚没有出现令人担忧的增长，但也没有证明质量提升。每个 PR 的行数上升也让结果更复杂：更多合并的修改可能带来更多审查工作。
  • 在加 token 限制或投入成本优化之前，先把可能的效果与提升吞吐量进行比较。每月一起追踪每个合并 PR 的总成本、支出、吞吐量和质量。
