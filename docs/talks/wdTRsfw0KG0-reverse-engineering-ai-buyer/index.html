DON'T DEFAULT TO A POC
POCs are the exception, not the default

FIRST, FIND THE REAL OBJECTION
Most concerns clear faster than a POC. Before you build one, try:
• A reference customer who had the same worry
• A guided eval on a slice of their data
• The trust portal for security objections
• An opt-out clause
• A “one year” POC

RESERVE A FULL POC FOR
The biggest, most complex deals, where the buyer genuinely can’t say yes without proof on their own stack.

If you run one: time-box it, define success up front, and charge for it.

A POC is expensive for both sides. Earn it, don’t default to it.

逆向工程 AI 买家

Aliisa Rosenthal 借鉴 OpenAI 的企业增长经验，讲解了产品获取方式、销售自动化、试点和定价如何影响采用——以及人工帮助在哪些地方仍然物有所值。

01

先建好机器，再组建团队

OpenAI already had a working buying motion when Aliisa Rosenthal joined: developers found the self-serve API, swiped a credit card and started building. Revenue was a couple million dollars. Over the following years, Rosenthal helped grow an organization of several hundred people and enterprise revenue to several billion. Bringing this “somewhat alien technology” into companies exposed a recurring problem: the way a product gets bought can obstruct the way people want to use it.

The conventional sales playbook starts by hiring salespeople, revenue operations and sales engineers. Only afterward does the organization find bottlenecks and add automation. Rosenthal reverses that sequence. Automate what can work without a person, observe where the process breaks, and hire people to get through those specific bottlenecks. The resulting team has a reason to exist beyond reproducing the usual sales organization.

02

企业需求掩盖了更大的自助市场

ChatGPT launched at the end of 2022 without enterprise features. Employees were already using it, but their organizations needed single sign-on, a nondisclosure agreement and an invoice. Those requests concerned the machinery around adoption: a useful product still needed a way to fit into company purchasing and administration.

After nine months of requests, the enterprise effort received approval. The largest companies had been the loudest and most successful at reaching Rosenthal, so their requirements shaped the offering. OpenAI built a fast, expensive product with extensive features and went aggressively upmarket. That selection process made large-enterprise demand unusually visible while leaving buyers who preferred a simpler purchase less represented.

The enterprise business performed well. Then, in January 2024, about four months after the enterprise version, a self-serve option arrived. Buyers could enter at a lower price without a heavy sales process. The mid-market and small-business motion grew much faster, and some customers preferred the cheaper option. Sales representatives now competed with their own company’s self-serve funnel. Rosenthal describes this as cannibalization; the account establishes the direction of the change without quantifying its size.

This example changes the order of product discovery. A self-serve launch first would have revealed what customers could accomplish independently and what they still lacked. Those missing capabilities could then justify a more expensive enterprise tier, followed by a team to sell it. Rosenthal says subsequent OpenAI product launches followed that sequence: start with self-service, learn where human help belongs, and add it.

03

一万条入站请求仍然需要一个答复

Early inbound demand overwhelmed Rosenthal and four sales representatives: roughly ten thousand requests arrived each day. A “champagne problem,” perhaps, but demand did not stay available indefinitely. OpenAI used Clay, which Rosenthal found useful, yet catching up with lead handling later could not restore information or relationships the initial process had failed to capture.

Two separate mechanisms would have helped:

- Capture information for follow-up: An optional, detailed sales form could collect requirements and a phone number from buyers willing to provide them. Once the team had capacity, it would have a way to reconnect. - Acknowledge demand immediately: An automated email could explain that the enterprise product was not ready, confirm a place on the waiting list and ask what the buyer needed. That would keep the relationship active during the delay.

When the enterprise product finally launched, almost every company Rosenthal contacted told her it had received no response and had bought Microsoft Copilot. Silence had become part of the buying experience. The practical lesson is to make an answer possible before making a full sales conversation possible.

Outbound has a similar division of labor. Researching prospects, sequencing outreach and dialing can be automated so representatives spend their time on qualified leads and valuable conversations. Clay appears here as a tool OpenAI used; Nooks appears as a tool Rosenthal had heard good things about. The useful distinction is between preparing a conversation and spending a person’s attention on it.

04

让买家持续推进——并避免把同一笔交易卖两次

A sales conversation can stall the moment the buyer leaves with homework. Filling out a form, integrating something or finding another person’s answer creates work outside the seller’s immediate reach. Instead of asking a buyer to complete five tasks and return, help complete them together: run a live Zoom session, organize a hackathon or work in the customer’s office. Shared work keeps the next step moving rather than waiting for the buyer to restart it.

The same principle applies to purchasing. Fewer pricing choices, fast paperwork and manageable approvals reduce the work required to say yes. A pilot can introduce the opposite pattern: sell the pilot, invest heavily in supervising it, then run another sales process to secure the actual purchase. “Pilot hell” consumes resources even when the trial succeeds.

Reserve that effort for the largest, highest-value opportunities. Buyers still need to validate that a product works in their environment, but a supported trial is only one way to satisfy that need:

- Customer reference: Introduce another customer who can discuss the product’s use. - Limited evaluation: Evaluate a portion of the buyer’s data or demonstrate the product with custom data over Zoom, without handing over ongoing access. - Contract with an exit: Sign the agreement with a ninety-day opt-out if the product does not meet the customer’s needs. - Shorter commitment: Offer a one-year proof of concept against a normal three-year contract.

Why does the ninety-day opt-out change the cycle? In a conventional supported pilot, the vendor invests in validation before winning the purchase. With a signed contract and an exit window, the customer must validate the product while its opportunity to opt out is still open. The diagram shows that change in timing. It moves commitment earlier and puts a deadline on the customer’s evaluation. Rosenthal presents these flexible terms as tactics available to startups, rather than terms a giant company would routinely offer.

Where commitment falls in the evaluation cycle A supported pilot can require a second sale after validation. A contract with a ninety-day opt-out places validation inside an already signed agreement. - Sell the pilot — Win agreement to begin a supported trial. - Manage validation — Invest team resources in running the pilot. - Sell the purchase — Run another sales process after a successful pilot. - Sign the contract — Include an exit if the product does not meet the customer's needs. - Customer validates — The customer evaluates while the opt-out clock runs. - Sell the pilot → Manage validation (Begin supported trial) - Manage validation → Sell the purchase (Success starts another sale) - Sign the contract → Customer validates (Ninety days to opt out)

05

消除安全审查带来的延误，降低进入成本

Security review is another place where deals stall. A trust portal lets customers sign an NDA and retrieve penetration-test reports and security documentation themselves. AI tools can also fill out security questionnaires. These mechanisms automate the repeated exchange of information around security review. Before scheduling a lengthy call, direct the buyer to the portal and ask which unanswered question still requires a conversation.

Pricing created a different obstacle. Rosenthal recalls an initial ChatGPT Enterprise price of sixty dollars per user per month, set largely around the cost of serving the high-end product. Lower-priced competing offerings exposed the problem. Her analogy is “building the Porsche before the Prius”: the expensive offering arrived before a more accessible entry point.

Follow the buying decision through its change. At sixty dollars per user per month, organizations restricted purchases to a subset of employees, such as developers or investors. Each additional employee required another substantial fixed commitment. OpenAI later moved to a lower base license fee plus usage charges. More companies signed agreements, access spread more widely within companies and usage increased over time. The entry decision became easier, while spending could grow with actual use.

Usage pricing introduces its own objection: the bill can climb as adoption grows. A dashboard with a monthly spending cap, or caps for particular employees, gives buyers a way to contain that exposure. Rosenthal observes that many companies like knowing the control exists even when they do not use it. Usage ideally corresponds to value, but the pricing mechanism alone does not establish that every additional unit of use is worthwhile.

06

牛排晚餐的复仇

The signal to hire salespeople is a repeated limit in an otherwise working process. Buyers need a demo, a conversation or someone they can look in the eye before trusting the purchase. Larger companies are more likely to require that interaction. Hiring then addresses a demonstrated obstacle rather than an assumed need for a large team.

Large deals can also distort priorities. An enterprise offering a million or ten million dollars is tempting, but serving it draws on legal, security, product, engineering and sales resources. Be selective about the first large partners. The contract’s revenue is only one side of the decision; the company must also live with the work and product direction that customer requires.

The preferred salesperson uses AI themselves, understands it and can sell it credibly. Automation can handle outbound, demos and security checklists while leaving buyers hungry for time with a person. Conferences, booths and dinners create those conversations. Rosenthal calls this “the revenge of the steak dinner”: human contact retains a job even as more surrounding work becomes automated.

Compensation should match the kind of seller the company needs. OpenAI grew a large sales organization without commission plans during Rosenthal’s account, helped by equity packages and the company’s increasing value. That experience does not make commission-free hiring a general model. The first two or three sales hires may be builders motivated by equity and the chance to shape a company. Experienced enterprise sellers are harder to recruit without variable cash compensation. Keep the eventual plan simple and give outperformers upside.

The remaining human task is value selling: connect the product to what changes for its buyer or internal champion. A feature list describes the tool; a useful sales conversation explains what the tool lets that person accomplish. Rosenthal considers humans better at making that connection than AI at the time of the talk. That is the substantive reason to keep the dinners and conversations, beyond familiarity with traditional sales.

07

自助试用和派驻工程的成本各不相同

The Q&A sharpens what avoiding pilots means. Product-led growth lets customers enter and use a product themselves. Access can be limited by consumption, time or available features. The objection concerns a contracted, services-heavy proof of concept that needs a salesperson or sales engineer to supervise it. Both let customers try the product, but only the latter commits substantial team attention to each evaluation.

Forward-deployed engineers add another services tradeoff. They are expensive and difficult to hire, yet helping integrate a product into a customer’s environment can make it harder to leave. The customer gains a customized tool that works for its particular organization. Rosenthal would prefer a product that works without this help, but sees good deployed engineers as an investment that can pay through a longer customer relationship. Her hope that continual learning might eventually remove this integration burden remains a forecast.

08

前十个客户需要不同的打法

The final question places an important limit on the automation-first advice. A startup seeking its first ten customers should treat them as design partners who need much more hands-on attention. Automated outbound belongs later, once the company has moved beyond those initial partnerships, has some product-market fit and is starting to scale. Early customer work must generate feedback about what the product should become.

Choose partners who will collaborate well and understand the product’s actual maturity, including its enterprise features. A recognizable company can be useful without placing the experiment in its main production system. Working on an internal or less critical product may make that customer willing to accept more risk and a lighter security process. The relationship can provide a recognizable customer logo and open the door to a larger agreement later.

Existing relationships are ideal for these early experiments: former customers or colleagues, investor introductions and friends inside companies. Friendly partners make room for a product that is still developing. The closing distinction is about stage. Spend personal attention where the company needs to learn, then automate the repeatable buying process and add people where customers still get stuck.

要点总结

  • 从自助和自动化开始；用它们的失败和客户反馈来决定要增加哪些企业级能力和人工角色。
  • 立即回应入站需求，并帮助买家一起完成困难的下一步。沉默和布置作业都会让购买周期跑偏。
  • 把有人监督的试点留给值得其资源成本的机会。参考客户、有限范围的评估和带退出条款的合同，可以以不同方式满足验证需求。
  • 更低的基础费加上按量计费可以扩大覆盖面，而公司和员工层面的支出上限则应对账单增长的风险。
  • 在建立信任、价值销售和针对客户的集成上，人的帮助依然重要。最初的设计合作伙伴也比一个可重复的销售漏斗需要更多关注。

  • Mastering AI Pricing — Mayank Pant, Stripe —— 探讨“订阅加按量”的定价、可变的算力成本，以及反映客户价值的计费单位。
