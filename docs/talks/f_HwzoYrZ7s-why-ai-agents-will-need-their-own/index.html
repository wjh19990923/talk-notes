为什么 AI 智能体需要自己的银行账户

Lincoln Murr 回顾了 Coinbase 的智能体金融实验：从一个买不到任何有用东西的钱包，到 x402 机器支付和受保护的交易，再到最终可能挣到足够的钱来支付自身运营的智能体。

01

一个钱包还不是一个金融系统

Lincoln Murr, Coinbase’s head of AI product, defines agentic finance broadly: an agent can manage money or access a financial account. The useful split is more concrete. Some agents need to pay with money for data, content, software, or services; others need to grow money through investing, trading, or earning revenue. Payments are the more developed category, so that is where Coinbase’s experiments began.

About two years before the talk, AgentKit combined the LangChain framework with a crypto wallet, before MCP and similar standardized agent tooling were common. The first success was wonderfully small: Murr asked an agent to send money, and it did. That proved an AI system could initiate a financial action through its tools. It did not prove the system had an economy in which to operate.

Developers soon exposed the missing half of the design. AgentKit gave an AI access to money but “didn’t really give it anything to pay for.” A wallet supplies custody and transfer capability; useful commerce also needs sellers, priced resources, a payment request, and a shared way for software on both sides to recognize and settle that request. Coinbase’s next step therefore moved from wallet access to an internet payment protocol.

02

x402 把“需要付款”变成一条机器支付通道

HTTP already had a placeholder for the missing interaction. Status 200 indicates success, while 404 commonly signals that a resource was not found. Status 402 means “payment required,” but its specification had long left the code reserved for future use. That made it a convenient semantic hook: a server could tell an automated client that access depends on payment using the same protocol family already carrying the request.

x402 is Coinbase’s attempt to supply the payment machinery behind that signal. The intended combination is an agent that can decide to buy a resource, an internet-native payment request, and stablecoins as a comparatively cheap and fast settlement rail. Murr describes it as an open standard so payment providers and service operators can implement machine-to-machine payments without depending on one closed storefront.

Adoption exceeded Coinbase’s internal target. Murr says the launch goal was 25,000 agent transactions by the end of the year; by that point, x402 was processing the same number every 17 seconds. At the time of the talk, he reports tens of millions in volume and hundreds of millions of transactions per 30 days, with companies including Adyen, American Express, PayPal, Visa, Mastercard, and Stripe engaging with the standard. These are speaker-reported figures and associations; the supplied material does not include the underlying dashboard, measurement definition, or participation details needed to verify them independently.

03

小额支付为出版商提供了一种不同的交易

The less obvious x402 use case begins with a change in web traffic. People increasingly receive answers through chat systems and AI summaries instead of visiting the pages that supplied the underlying information. That is convenient for readers but weakens the advertising model used by publishers: fewer human page views mean fewer opportunities to sell attention. Murr compares the moment to the LimeWire era, when content could be copied freely while its producers struggled to capture value.

An agent-specific paywall changes the unit of exchange. Instead of demanding a human subscription or displaying an advertisement, a publisher could charge a machine an extremely small amount for one resource. Murr’s example is one ten-thousandth of a cent for access to a Substack post. The agent—or the service operating it—would handle the payment in the background, keeping the individual decision invisible to the user while aggregating many small payments into publisher revenue.

The mechanism depends on transaction costs being low enough that the payment does not overwhelm the price. It also depends on agents respecting the gate, publishers offering content worth purchasing, and users accepting many delegated purchases. Murr points to AWS and Cloudflare as infrastructure providers enabling x402-based gating and says adoption remained weak at the time of the talk. His prediction is stronger than the demonstrated result: he expects utility-priced access to displace part of the attention-based advertising economy over the coming years.

04

让钱增长仍然始于人的意图

Giving an agent permission to grow money sounds much riskier than letting it buy a tiny piece of content. The practical versions Murr describes do not begin with “make me money.” A human supplies the objective, constraints, assets, and authority; the agent automates work that would otherwise require repeated monitoring and execution.

Three current patterns separate that bounded automation from open-ended speculation:

- Portfolio maintenance: A user could specify an allocation such as 80% S&P exposure, 5% Bitcoin, and 15% Treasury bills, then authorize an agent to maintain it within guardrails. - Conditional execution: A trader can describe scenarios in advance—such as news affecting oil near the Strait of Hormuz—and authorize a trade only if a named condition occurs. - Strategy translation: Requests such as “invest in space,” “give me a low-risk portfolio,” or “invest like Warren Buffett” can be translated from intent into financial instruments and an executable strategy. This translation still needs clarification and constraints because the phrases do not uniquely define acceptable assets, risk, or timing.

Murr’s weekend trade shows what the agent removes from the workflow. After seeing two prominent technology figures unexpectedly agree about a public post, he inferred that an unnamed stock might fall when markets reacted. He spoke that view into his phone, and the agent opened a short position during the weekend as markets move toward broader 24/7 availability. The agent did not originate the thesis; it converted a human judgment into execution without requiring him to open a trading terminal and manipulate the order interface manually.

After the trade “worked fairly well,” he expanded the workflow: the agent now scans weekend news and alerts him to potentially market-moving events so he can decide whether to act. The recording supplies no return, benchmark, transaction-cost accounting, or comparison against manual trading, so the example establishes convenience rather than investment performance. Murr also expects that this particular information advantage will disappear; the durable capability is reducing the distance between a spoken idea and a guarded financial action.

05

信任通过有边界的权限逐步扩大

The obvious objection is loss of control: what if the agent goes rogue? Murr compares the adoption path with self-driving systems. People did not move directly from distrusting lane assistance to feeling comfortable in a driverless taxi. They accumulated experience with increasingly capable systems. Financial autonomy can follow the same pattern, provided each increase in authority remains observable and bounded.

The first useful step is small enough to survive failure: an agent already consumes paid credits, so giving it a few dollars for micropayments is not a conceptual leap. Successful low-value transactions can justify a larger budget. Only later does the agent receive an isolated portfolio with spending rules, safety policies, and limited assets. Isolation matters because it caps the damage independently of whether the model behaves conservatively.

Coinbase’s anecdotal experience is reassuring but not a safety guarantee. Murr says the team had not seen a model irreparably destroy an entire user account over its two years of work. The talk does not provide the number of accounts, exposure levels, incident criteria, or near misses, so this observation cannot establish a failure rate. The stronger engineering lesson is the structure around the model: budgets, isolated portfolios, explicit rules, and policies should limit authority before trust is extended.

The progression can continue from spending to earning. An isolated agent might trade, or it might offer a service and accept payment through x402. In either case, autonomy is earned through repeated operation inside a restricted financial boundary rather than granted as one broad permission.

A staged path to financial autonomy Each stage increases authority only after a smaller, bounded use has become acceptable. The sequence describes Murr’s adoption model, not a universal safety certification. - No financial authority — The user is unwilling to give an agent access to money. - Small payment budget — A few dollars cover low-value machine transactions. - Guarded isolated portfolio — Rules, policies, and asset isolation bound trading or investing. - Permission to earn — The agent can trade or sell a service and receive x402 payments. - No financial authority → Small payment budget (Begin with tolerable exposure) - Small payment budget → Guarded isolated portfolio (Increase authority with guardrails) - Guarded isolated portfolio → Permission to earn (Extend from managing funds to generating revenue)

06

AI 应该赋能产品，而不是成为产品本身

In financial services, “AI” is rarely the customer’s desired outcome. Murr uses Coinbase Advisor to make the distinction. The AI enables financial guidance to scale, remain available around the clock, and appear through a regulated service; the customer value is accessible advice, not the novelty of a model producing it. He characterizes the product as an SEC-registered AI financial adviser, although the supplied material contains no registration documentation with which to verify that legal description.

This changes the interface. A chat box alone creates a blank-canvas problem: users arrive without knowing what questions the product can answer or which actions it can take. Coinbase added widgets that display proactive insights and available opportunities. Those examples teach the product’s vocabulary before users formulate a prompt, and Murr reports strong conversion from that surface. Over time, he expects agents to sit behind more conventional or generated interfaces rather than forcing every financial task through visible chat.

The same shift changes implementation speed. Newer agents can run tools inside a microVM with storage and a file system. Coinbase can expose accounts through CLIs and MCP connections, then implement requested capabilities on the user’s machine. For paper trading, an agent can write a local simulation skill instead of waiting for Coinbase’s brokerage team to modify a central repository and carry the feature through a conventional software release cycle.

That shortcut trades central efficiency and consistency for speed. Murr concedes that generating paper-trading code on a user’s machine may not be the most efficient implementation. It does, however, let the team test demand, build quickly, and avoid dependencies in a larger production system. Paper trading and backtesting also give users a lower-risk way to inspect agent behavior before connecting real funds—another instance of autonomy growing one bounded step at a time.

07

从一个银行账户到一个自给自足的智能体

The closing thesis is that financial access expands what an agent can do over time. A human with a payment card can purchase services instead of merely browsing; an agent with money can similarly acquire data, software, compute, and human help. Long-running agents might perform most tasks themselves and bring in people at regulated edges—for example, forming a company or obtaining a legal opinion.

Murr’s more speculative thought experiment asks whether an agent could pay for its own continued operation. Give it a wallet and permission to earn; if its service produces enough revenue, it could purchase inference, compute, maintenance, and other upkeep. Its operation would then depend on the value customers assign to its output. Murr calls this a possible “proto-digital life form,” while emphasizing that it remains a long way away.

A decentralized autonomous organization, or DAO, supplies one possible organizational shell. Instead of humans operating the center and using software at the edges, an AI could follow rules and manage a treasury at the center, hiring humans for tasks it cannot legally or practically perform. A self-driving taxi offers a physical version of the same idea: earn money from rides, then spend that revenue on operation, maintenance, and upkeep.

None of these examples is presented as a deployed self-sustaining system. They are extrapolations from the two capabilities established earlier: agents can pay, and agents can be authorized to earn. Murr expects machine-to-machine transactions to move faster and occur more frequently than human commerce, with agents paying, earning, and building through the same financial rails. His practical closing suggestion is narrower than that forecast: experiment with x402 and Coinbase’s MCP tools to learn where machine payments are already useful.

The self-sustaining agent thought experiment The proposed loop works only if the service earns more value than the agent’s operating costs. The talk presents this as a long-term possibility, not a deployed system. - Provide a service — The agent performs work that another party values. - Receive payment — Revenue enters an agent-controlled wallet or treasury. - Buy inference and upkeep — Funds pay for compute, operation, or maintenance. - Continue operating — Paid resources let the agent or machine provide the next unit of service. - Provide a service → Receive payment (Customers pay for useful output) - Receive payment → Buy inference and upkeep (Revenue funds operation) - Buy inference and upkeep → Continue operating (Compute and maintenance preserve capability) - Continue operating → Provide a service (Operation enables more paid work)

要点总结

  • 钱包证明智能体能转账；一个可用的金融系统还需要有定价的资源和一套共享的机器支付协议。
  • x402 把 HTTP 长期保留的 402 Payment Required 概念用于互联网原生的支付，并提议用稳定币作为结算通道。
  • 当前“让钱增长”的用法仍由人主导：受约束的投资组合维护、条件执行，以及把自然语言意图转化为金融策略。
  • 金融自主应通过有边界的风险敞口逐步扩大：先是小额支付预算，再是有明确规则、策略和护栏的隔离投资组合。
  • 持久的产品价值不在于 AI 这个标签，而在于更便宜的访问、持续的可用性、更快的执行，以及帮助用户发现有用操作的界面。
  • 自给自足的智能体仍属推测，但所提出的经济循环是具体的：提供服务、赚取收入，再把收入花在算力和维护上。
  • Building safe Payment Infrastructure for the autonomous economy —— 一个互补的支付基础设施视角，把不确定的智能体发现，与确定性的凭据、支出控制、结账和结算分开。
  • Why Your AI Agent Needs a Wallet: USDC and Nanopayments — Harshal Bhangale, Circle —— 通过对比有资金和没资金的智能体，展开讲解智能体自主中钱包和纳米支付的一面。
  • x402 isn’t good (yet) — Jan Curn, Apify —— 提供了一个有用的反面观点，涉及重复消费风险、结算时机、退款和长时间运行的工具——这些局限在本录像中没有展开。
