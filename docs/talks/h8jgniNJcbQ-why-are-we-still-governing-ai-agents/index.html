为什么我们还在像管理软件一样管理 AI 智能体？

Gravitee 的 Tim Tyler 围绕身份、一位具名的人类发起人、有边界的委托授权和持续监督，提出了一个“智能体问责”框架。它的核心区分很实际：一个智能体可能始终没有越权，却仍然没能做好它的工作。

01

一份没人会批准的岗位描述

A marketing-operations job requires no identification, has no manager, and offers only an obscure description of the work. The new hire gets access to key systems through shared passwords, works around the clock, receives no supervision, and faces no consequences for mistakes. Tim Tyler of Gravitee opens with this deliberately unreasonable vacancy. Nobody in the audience raises a hand to approve it. Yet these are the conditions he sees organizations creating for AI agents.

The organizational gap appears in a conversation Tyler recounts with an AI leader: the CTO does not quite consider agents software applications, while HR considers them precisely that. Responsibility falls between departments. Protocols, standards, and conventional software controls remain necessary; the proposed addition is a way to govern delegated work and identify who answers for it.

The Agent Accountability Framework borrows structures from managing a human workforce and adapts them to agents. The talk develops three stages: Assignment establishes the agent's place in the organization; Alignment defines its work and permitted scope; Administration checks outcomes and takes action when behavior departs from expectations. These are organizing principles backed by auditing controls, rather than a particular agent architecture.

02

分配：给智能体一个身份和一个负责的人

Assignment starts with an ID badge—ideally without the photograph that competes with your DMV picture for the worst of your life. The technical requirement beneath the joke is one unique agent ID in a known, shared registry that acts as the single source of truth. Every other part of the framework points back to that identity. Without it, the organization cannot consistently attach a role, permissions, or responsibility to the same agent.

The agent also belongs to a team, even when that team consists of just one human and one agent. Team membership supplies context: goals, previous decisions, and history. Information must travel back as well, so humans and other agents know what the agent is doing and learning. Calling it a teammate preserves an important distinction: acting on a person's behalf does not make the agent that person.

Agent 409 makes the responsibility concrete. Its sponsor is a named person, Sven. He defines its purpose, owns what it costs and spends, and gets the call when something goes wrong. That name connects the agent to the organization's existing management hierarchy. A team name alone would leave unanswered which person must respond.

Tyler's pool-safety rule explains what accepting sponsorship means. When he needed another adult to watch his children, he made eye contact and asked, “Do you have the kids?” The other adult had to answer, “I have the kids.” Responsibility transferred through an explicit acknowledgment. Sven's equivalent is “I have the agent”: ownership must be accepted, not merely assumed because several people are nearby.

03

对齐：把常设角色与当前任务分开

Once the organization knows who Agent 409 is and who answers for it, the next question is what it should do. A vague job description forces a human employee to infer expectations and check with colleagues. The framework instead gives the agent an explicit purpose, responsibilities for accomplishing it, a budget, and a definition of success. Those details provide something against which later work can be judged.

Two definitions operate at different timescales:

- Role at onboarding: Sven defines why the agent exists and its standing responsibilities. This changes rarely, unless subsequent intervention requires a revision. - Mission at runtime: The agent defines what it is trying to accomplish at the current moment—for example, when making a call to an MCP tool.

The distinction keeps the agent's immediate choice of work separate from the sponsor's definition of its job. Defining a mission does not transfer ownership of the role to the agent.

04

委派必须保留调用者的权限范围

A role explains the job; authority determines which data and tools the agent can use to perform it. Sven decides which doors Agent 409 may open. IT, a database team, or an MCP-server team may implement the access, but the accountable sponsor decides that the agent needs it. This is also where limits and escalation conditions are defined.

What happens when the agent receiving a delegated task has more access than the agent making the request? Tyler uses three permission sets to make the relationship inspectable. Sven can access A, B, C, D. Agent 203, which discusses projects with him, can access C, D. Agent 409 can access D, E, F. Agent 203 delegates a task to Agent 409 on Sven's behalf.

In this example, Agent 409 may use D, E, F to do its work, but only D can return up the delegation chain. The common scope is {A, B, C, D} ∩ {C, D} ∩ {D, E, F} = {D}. Sven's access to A and B does not grant those permissions to the agents; Agent 409's access to E and F does not make that information available to Sven through Agent 203. The outward task delegation and the returning information therefore need separate treatment. The talk specifies the intended scope relationship, without detailing how a system would enforce it on derived results.

Returning information is limited to D, the only scope shared by every participant; Agent 409's local access to E and F does not expand the upstream caller's access. - Sven — Access: A, B, C, D - Agent 203 — Access: C, D. Discusses projects with Sven and delegates to Agent 409. - Agent 409 — Access: D, E, F. 05

发起人并不能控制影响智能体的每一个因素

“Never assume alignment” follows directly from the limits of sponsorship. Agent 409 inherits behavior from its underlying model, receives team context maintained elsewhere, and may interact with agents from other organizations. Sven cannot simply edit all of those influences. An outside agent should be treated like a contractor entering a building: give it an identity and authorization, including conditions on when it may access the organization, rather than assuming it shares the organization's intentions.

Sven can define Agent 409's own rules and intended outcomes. The rules provide a deterministic floor, such as fine-grained access policies. The intents describe what the agent should accomplish, where judging success may be probabilistic. This separates inherited alignment—which can change without Sven controlling it—from the defined expectations he can impose on Agent 409. Administration is the next stage because writing those expectations does not enforce them.

06

管理：守规矩和取得成功是两回事

An agent can stay within every access restriction and still do poor work. Authority defines acceptable behavior; the role defines success. Oversight must check both. That is why the framework calls for a scorecard rather than one metric: permission compliance cannot stand in for task quality, and a useful result cannot excuse unauthorized access.

What would the scorecard distinguish in invoice work?

- Authority checks: An illustrative agent may handle invoices only up to $40,000. Other example rules prohibit approving payments or permit writes only to the ledger. These are explicit constraints to check. - Role outcomes: Invoice-match accuracy and reconciliation cycle time measure whether the work is useful. Matching the correct purchase order, vendor, and project requires judging the result against the intended task, with a confidence level attached to that judgment.

The role and authority definitions supply the scorecard's weights. As Tyler puts it, “rules are checked, intents are judged.” The distinction makes a compliant but incorrect match visible as a failure rather than a successful authorized action.

Oversight also needs to inspect more than the final answer. The proposed guardian agents watch the model, the context entering the agent, and the work product coming out. Those are separate places where behavior can diverge from expectations. Continuous checks at machine speed are the point of the otherwise unwelcome management analogy: agents need micromanagers. A quarterly review can arrive after enormous numbers of decisions have already happened.

07

把不确定的判断变成定义好的动作

Detecting a problem is only useful if the organization has decided what happens next. The human-workforce analogy starts with correction rather than immediate dismissal: explain the misunderstanding, restrict authority if problems continue, escalate, and eventually stop the work. Agent recourse needs similarly explicit responses, with different treatment for uncertain task judgments and clear permission violations.

How can a probabilistic evaluation trigger a predictable response? Tyler sketches confidence-linked actions: at 90% confidence, the system might redact out-of-alignment content; below 50%, it escalates to a human; below 20%, it blocks. The mechanism is to map an uncertain assessment to a predefined action. These are illustrative thresholds, not a complete policy: the talk does not define how confidence is calibrated or resolve the overlap between the below-50% and below-20% conditions.

An explicit access violation offers a different basis for action. Authorization can be restricted immediately after an unauthorized access; access might be suspended after five violations in a day; persistent violations over several days might suspend the entire agent. Here the response depends on a known rule breach and its repetition, rather than confidence in a semantic judgment. The escalation policy makes both the current incident and the agent's history consequential.

Tyler's proposed zero-trust posture begins with heavier controls and lightens them after repeated success. That creates an ongoing operational decision, not a setting to configure once and forget. The practical requirement is fast enforcement combined with continued examination of whether the restrictions and outcomes remain appropriate.

08

机器负责执行，发起人负责调整工作

Guardian agents, AI gateways, and guardrails are proposed enforcement mechanisms: they restrict, prohibit, or suspend activity at machine speed. Sven has a different job. He uses human judgment to ask whether changing the role or authority definition would produce better work or better behavior. Enforcement acts on the current expectations; coaching revises those expectations when they are causing the wrong result.

The closing formulation is compact: name every agent, bound every action, trace every outcome. The framework supplies principles and auditing controls; technical systems provide ways to carry them out. The first practical move is to give agents their own identities, so the organization can attach the rest of that responsibility to something identifiable. And, in the spirit of every employee badge Tyler has disliked, give the agent the worst picture of its life.

要点总结

  • 在共享注册表中给每个智能体一个唯一的身份，并配一位具名的人类发起人，负责它的用途、成本，以及出问题时的响应。
  • 把发起人定义的常设角色与智能体当前的任务分开，并在整条委派链上保持权限限制，而不是把参与者的访问权限合并起来。
  • 既衡量合规，也衡量成功。发票权限可以直接检查，而采购订单匹配是否正确，需要一个附带置信度的任务质量判断。
  • 在失败发生之前定义追责方式：把不确定的评估和反复违规映射到相应的动作，自动化快速执行，并让人类发起人负责修订角色和权限。

延伸阅读
