你的 AI 分析师能保守秘密吗？

桥水的 Vault 存有约一亿份访问规则各不相同的文档。Aaron Linsky 和 Nikhil Benesch 解释了这些权限为什么会让向量检索变得复杂，以及 turbopuffer 如何把权限索引与向量聚类结合起来，让检索保持快速。

分析师示意图：一个问题被发送给语言模型，模型在两个集合中搜索。公开网络对所有人开放，私有文档则各自带有权限。

01

分析师的搜索结果取决于是谁在问

An AI analyst receives a question: why have token prices fallen so much in the last month? The model begins investigating and searches for facts to guide its next steps. Public web pages can enter that investigation regardless of which employee asked the question. Internal emails, meeting notes and research reports require a different decision: is this particular user allowed to see this particular document?

That is the search problem Aaron Linsky, head of product engineering at Bridgewater Associates, and Nikhil Benesch, CTO of turbopuffer, develop in this talk. A document can grant access to an individual, a group, or some combination of them. The analyst needs relevant evidence drawn from the documents its user can access. Finding nearby vectors is only part of that job; the search also has to respect a different permitted collection for each user.

02

Vault 把研究资料和权限放到一起

Bridgewater’s research process starts with observations about markets and economies and turns tested ideas into systems that trade. Its 1980 bond system was a set of if-then statements on a yellow legal pad. The implementation moved through Excel, Scala and Python, while the learning process kept humans at its center: perceive what is happening, form a hypothesis, investigate it, systemize and integrate successful ideas, then industrialize them so they run every day.

The Vault example shows a user with an ID and group memberships beside three documents. Highlighted entries distinguish a direct grant from access through a group.

Moving machines toward the center of that process makes access to research material essential. Bridgewater has spent decades building DeLorean, its bitemporal macro time series database. The Vault supplies the unstructured side: about 100 million documents that Bridgewater indexes, organizes and secures. It combines research from 135 external sources with internal writing, meeting recordings and reports. External sources have their own access restrictions, so bringing material into one database does not make it available to everyone.

Investors and AI agents both read from the Vault, and the new content they create goes back into it. Search therefore serves an expanding research collection. In Linsky’s simplified permissions example, user 1 belongs to groups B and C. A document may be accessible through a direct grant to that user or through one of those groups. A vector query has to combine similarity with those access conditions.

The first implementation appeared to work. Then the growing document collection overwhelmed it—Linsky’s description is that it “fell over.” He looked for other providers and tested turbopuffer. The failure establishes the practical requirement for the rest of the talk: permission logic that looks simple in a query must remain affordable when the collection reaches tens of millions of documents.

03

廉价的存储仍然需要一种跳过工作的方法

turbopuffer stores data directly in object storage such as S3, GCS or Azure Blob Storage. Data begins cold there; queries cause the needed data to move into SSD or memory caches. An untouched namespace—or an untouched region within a namespace—can stay in cheaper storage. Frequently accessed data becomes hot and can serve faster searches. Benesch attributes the system’s cost advantage to this arrangement, with the performance comparison to traditional disk-based search systems explicitly depending on the data being hot.

The engine supports keyword, regular-expression and vector search. Benesch focuses on vector search to explain the indexing problem, beginning with the simplest possible implementation: store everything in a list and inspect every item for every query. His engineering preference is to earn complexity with napkin math before introducing it.

Assume each document occupies only one kilobyte. Even an embedding can consume that much: 1,024 dimensions at one byte per dimension already amounts to roughly a kilobyte, before larger document contents enter the calculation. At the assumed scan rate of 20 gigabytes per second, 100 million one-kilobyte documents take about five seconds to inspect, even when all the data is resident in memory. That misses the roughly 100-millisecond response time Benesch says search users commonly want.

At 10,000 documents, the same calculation gives about half a millisecond, so a scan can be perfectly reasonable. Scale changes the decision. At 10 million or 100 million documents, the representation must let a query avoid most of the collection. For 100 million documents, the table estimates 50 seconds for “S3 (many)” at 2 GiB/s and 1,000 seconds for “S3 (1)” at 100 MiB/s. A cache hit avoids fetching that data from S3. An index reduces how much data the query needs to access.

04

向量搜索缩小了邻域

An embedding represents a concept numerically so that similar concepts can lie near one another. Red shoes and maroon sandals make an intuitive pair; a concrete block is much less similar. The teaching example reduces vectors to two dimensions so they can be plotted. Those axes are not established semantic labels such as size and color—Benesch offers those only as an intuition. Real embeddings commonly have 1,024 dimensions, and may have thousands.

What work does the index let the query avoid? In the two-dimensional example, red shoes and maroon sandals occupy the upper-left region, while concrete block lies toward the lower right. A query marked Q for red shoes can search its local neighborhood instead of examining the whole plot. Nearby vectors supply plausible results because proximity represents conceptual similarity. The spatial relationship explains the saving: searching around Q avoids spending the same effort on distant concepts.

turbopuffer builds a vector index automatically once the stored data is large enough to justify it. A query might then inspect only 10,000 vectors, which Benesch estimates can take a millisecond or two. Permissions disturb this useful shortcut. In the next version of the example, plus signs mark documents the user may access and minus signs mark documents they may not. A neighborhood can be close to Q yet contain few usable results.

05

在搜索之前或之后过滤会产生不同的失败

Two straightforward strategies choose a different starting point:

- Post-filtering: Find nearby vectors first—perhaps the closest 10,000—then discard documents whose permissions do not match. - Pre-filtering: Find every document the user can access first, then scan those documents to determine which vectors are closest to the query.

Why can a nearest-neighbor search miss the nearest accessible results? Suppose the user can access only documents in the lower-right region of the example. Searching around Q first spends its effort on forbidden points. Expanding outward may eventually reach accessible documents, but the search has a time budget. It can stop after a couple of seconds with only 40 of the requested top 100 results—or with none. A user searching for red shoes may know that an accessible maroon-sandals document exists and still fail to retrieve it. This is the recall problem: relevant, permitted documents are missing from the returned set.

The pre-filtering example connects the query to accessible documents across the plot. The query plan calculates their distances before selecting the nearest results.

Pre-filtering avoids spending similarity calculations on forbidden documents, but the permitted set can itself be enormous. If Aaron can access 50 million of the Vault’s 100 million documents, scanning the permitted half brings back the original bandwidth problem. The filter index has found the right population; it has not used vector proximity to make that population cheap to search. One strategy risks running out of useful candidates, while the other risks doing far too much work.

06

原生过滤在搜索邻近簇之前先去掉空簇

turbopuffer combines the two approaches through a clustered vector index. Similar vectors are grouped together. The permission indexes also identify which clusters contain at least one document the user may access. That gives the query a useful middle level between individual documents and the entire collection: it can eliminate a whole cluster when none of that cluster’s documents could become a permitted result.

Which clusters survive for Aaron? In Benesch’s example, clusters 5 and perhaps 2 contain documents he can access, while clusters 3 and 0 contain none. The latter can be discarded before similarity search. Among the surviving clusters, the engine searches the closest ones and scans sequentially inside them. A surviving cluster means that at least one document qualifies; it does not grant access to every document in the cluster. The combination still has to return documents that satisfy the permission filter.

In the native filtering example, the query plan drops clusters without accessible documents, then searches nearby qualifying clusters and checks individual documents.

turbopuffer calls this native filtering. Searching only selected clusters saves work, but it can miss a permitted document that belongs in the nearest results. The slide shows about 95% recall for native filtering. Scanning every matching document considers every permitted candidate.

At the time of the talk, clusters contain about 200 documents. Benesch describes that size as a tuning choice and expects it may increase to maximize throughput. The essential mechanism is the combination of permission-aware cluster selection and local scanning; 200 is the reported configuration, not a necessary constant of the design.

07

访问规则由应用来提供

The reported result is 120 milliseconds at P90 across the deployment’s namespaces, for a collection of about 100 million documents. P90 describes the latency at the ninetieth percentile, rather than a maximum for every query. The deployment held hundreds of millions of vectors. The application-facing question remains much simpler than the indexing work: how does user 1’s membership in B and C become a search filter? The displayed example uses viewers Contains 1 and groups ContainsAny B C.

The displayed query combines the permission rule with a deletion check.

``text eligible(document) = document.deleted equals false AND ( document.viewers contains 1 OR document.groups contains any of [B, C] ) ``

Either a direct grant or a grant through one of the user’s groups satisfies the permission rule. The deletion check applies to both paths.

The application expresses that permission logic in turbopuffer’s filter language, and the engine handles query planning underneath it. This division lets the application retain its conceptual access model without implementing the clustered search strategy itself. Benesch describes searches in the roughly 100–200-millisecond range even at 100-million-document scale.

The slide identifies two costs of maintaining the permission data. Nested groups amplify the metadata updates the application must push to turbopuffer. Changes in group membership can require rewriting all affected documents, which the slide describes as slow. Benesch invites teams with nested groups to work through them with turbopuffer.

08

权限是搜索隔离的一个层级

The ending widens the requirement from document visibility to what Benesch calls an “isolation-native search engine.” Fine-grained permissions operate within namespaces, but organizations can also need controls over where data resides and who controls its protection:

- Regional placement: Keep data in a particular region to meet security or regulatory obligations. - Network placement: Keep data inside the organization’s own VPC. - Encryption control: Support keys controlled by the organization, potentially extending that control to its customers.

Benesch presents turbopuffer as having solutions at each of these levels, though this talk explains the mechanics of permission filtering rather than those deployment controls. Bridgewater’s case makes the retrieval requirement concrete: an analyst needs a large shared research collection, yet its useful search space changes with the user. Native filtering makes those access rules part of deciding where to search, allowing the engine to skip work before the document scan becomes prohibitively large.

要点总结

  • 感知权限的检索必须在用户可访问的文档集合中找到相关文档。仅凭邻近的向量无法识别出可用的结果。
  • 当被禁止的文档占据了最近的邻域时，后过滤可能漏掉可访问的结果。前过滤则可能需要扫描数千万份被允许的文档。
  • 原生过滤去掉没有任何可访问文档的簇，搜索剩下的最近的簇，并在本地扫描时保留文档级的权限检查。
